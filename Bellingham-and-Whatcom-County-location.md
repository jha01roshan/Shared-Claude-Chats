## Human

E lives in Bellingham. E goes to school at a place called Whatcom community college. Do these two statements prove that Bellingham is in Whatcom County?

---

## Assistant

No, these two statements do not prove that Bellingham is in Whatcom County.

The logical gap is this: the statements tell you that E lives in Bellingham and attends **Whatcom Community College**, but they don't establish *where* Whatcom Community College is located. A college named after a county doesn't have to be physically situated in that county — and even if it were, that wouldn't tell you where Bellingham is.

To illustrate the flaw, consider a parallel example: someone lives in City X and attends "Riverside Community College." That tells you nothing about whether City X is in Riverside County — the college could be in a different city or county entirely, and the person could commute.

To logically prove that Bellingham is in Whatcom County, you'd need at least one additional premise, such as:
- "Whatcom Community College is located in Bellingham" (which would still only place the college in Bellingham, not prove the county), or more directly,
- "Bellingham is the county seat of Whatcom County," or
- Some geographic fact directly linking Bellingham to Whatcom County.

As it happens, Bellingham *is* in Whatcom County in real life — but that's background knowledge, not something the two original statements prove on their own.
