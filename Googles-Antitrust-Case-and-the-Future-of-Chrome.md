## Current Status of the Google Antitrust Case

Google is currently in court for the final phase of a landmark antitrust case that could force the company to spin off its Chrome browser business. This follows a ruling in August 2024 where a federal judge determined that Google holds an illegal monopoly in the search market.

The Justice Department and Google are now battling over how to fix this search engine monopoly in a remedies trial taking place in Washington D.C. The trial began on April 21, 2025, and is expected to last three weeks, concluding on May 9, with Judge Amit Mehta's ruling anticipated in August 2025.

## What the DOJ is Proposing

The Department of Justice is pushing for Google to divest its Chrome internet browser as a remedy. According to the DOJ filing, this would "permanently stop Google's control of this critical search access point."

In addition to the Chrome divestiture, the DOJ is calling on Google to end exclusive distribution agreements with phone makers like Apple and Samsung, through which Google paid them to establish its search engine as the default browser on their phones. The DOJ is also seeking to prevent Google from establishing similar exclusive distribution agreements for its AI programs and apps, like its Gemini chatbot.

## Google's Response

Google's Global Affairs President and Legal Chief Kent Walker called the DOJ's proposal "overbroad" and argued that it "goes miles beyond the Court's decision" and "would break a range of Google products — even beyond Search."

Google plans to argue that U.S. national security and innovation will be at risk if it's forced to split apart. The company claims that Chrome provides freedom, helps people access the web, and its open source code is used by other companies.

Google is also raising concerns about the proposed requirements to share online search data with rivals and the proposed sale of Chrome, claiming these actions pose privacy and security risks.

## Broader Context and Other Cases

This case represents one of the most notable antitrust cases in decades and the first antitrust win against Big Tech since Microsoft's antitrust settlement in 1998. Google is also dealing with another antitrust case related to its advertising technology.

On April 17, 2025, a District Judge in Virginia ruled that Google established and maintained monopoly power in open-web digital advertising markets by engaging in anticompetitive practices, marking the government's second victory in its antitrust battles against Google.

Whatever happens with these cases, the process is expected to take a long time. Even after the judge rules on remedies this summer, Google intends to appeal the original ruling that branded its search engine as an illegal monopoly.

This situation is still developing, with the remedies trial currently underway. The judge's decision, expected in August, will determine whether Google will have to sell Chrome, though Google plans to appeal regardless of the outcome.
