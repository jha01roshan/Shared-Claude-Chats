If you are a licensed attorney in the state of Kansas, practicing in the State of Kansas, and you believe you have committed a breach of the rules of professional conduct in Kansas, are you required to self report?

## Assistant

In Kansas, there is **no explicit requirement** for attorneys to self-report their own violations of the Rules of Professional Conduct.

Kansas Rule of Professional Conduct 8.3 governs the reporting of professional misconduct, but it specifically addresses when lawyers must report **other lawyers'** misconduct, not their own. Under Rule 8.3(a), a Kansas attorney must report another lawyer's conduct that raises a substantial question as to that lawyer's honesty, trustworthiness, or fitness as a lawyer.

However, there are important practical considerations:

1. **Duty of Candor**: If you're involved in disciplinary proceedings or judicial proceedings, you have duties of candor under other rules (like Rule 3.3 regarding candor to tribunals and Rule 8.1 regarding bar admission and disciplinary matters) that would require truthfulness.

2. **Rule 8.1**: This rule specifically prohibits knowingly making false statements or failing to disclose facts in connection with a disciplinary matter or bar admission application.

3. **Strategic Considerations**: While not legally required to self-report, some attorneys choose to do so proactively, particularly if the violation is likely to be discovered anyway, as voluntary disclosure and cooperation can be viewed favorably in mitigation.

4. **Corrective Action**: Some rules (like Rule 1.4 on communication or Rule 1.15 on safekeeping property) may require you to take corrective action to remedy harm, even if they don't require self-reporting the violation itself.

If you're concerned about a potential violation, consulting with an ethics attorney or the Kansas Bar Association's ethics hotline would be advisable to understand your specific obligations and options.
