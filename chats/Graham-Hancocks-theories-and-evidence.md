You're absolutely right to push back on that. I was presenting "the case Hancock supporters would make," but I phrased it too credulously.

The Younger Dryas Impact Hypothesis is genuinely contested, not settled science that happens to have peer-reviewed support. The reality is messier:

**Papers have been published supporting it** — nanodiamonds, platinum anomalies, melt glass, magnetic spherules at various sites. Firestone et al. (2007) kicked it off, and there have been follow-ups.

**But there's substantial published criticism too** — other teams couldn't replicate the nanodiamond findings, the spherules have alternative explanations, dating inconsistencies, some "impact proxies" appear at sites with wrong timing. The hypothesis has been declared dead multiple times by critics, only for supporters to publish more.

**Peer review guarantees process, not truth** — it means a paper met a threshold for publication, not that its conclusions are correct. Plenty of peer-reviewed work turns out to be wrong, unreplicable, or contested by subsequent peer-reviewed work. That's how science is supposed to function, but it's not the quality stamp people often assume.

So the honest framing: the YDIH has proponents publishing in journals, but it remains a minority position with serious unresolved criticisms. It's not "gaining traction" in the sense of approaching consensus—it's an ongoing dispute.
