Here's a selection of relevant papers from PubMed on the impact of exercise on cancer:

## Reviews and Mechanistic Studies

**Zhu et al. (2022)** provide a comprehensive review examining exercise in cancer prevention and anticancer therapy. The paper covers how exercise reduces cancer incidence, enhances efficacy of treatments (targeted therapy, immunotherapy, radiotherapy), and reduces symptoms like fatigue, cachexia, and cognitive impairment. Mechanisms discussed include intratumoral angiogenesis, myokines/adipokines, cancer metabolism, and anticancer immunity.  
[DOI: 10.1016/j.canlet.2022.215814](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.canlet.2022.215814)

**Feng et al. (2024)** published a recent review in *Frontiers in Immunology* on mechanistic perspectives, demonstrating that regular physical activity reduces cancer risk, mitigates therapy side effects, and impacts tumor behavior, immune responses, and metabolic pathways.  
[DOI: 10.3389/fimmu.2024.1474770](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2024.1474770)

**Idorn & Thor Straten (2017)** discuss the transition from exercise as "healthy" to potentially "therapeutic," describing how voluntary exercise leads to immune cell infiltration in tumors and a >60% reduction in tumor incidence and growth across mouse models—suggesting potential synergy with immunotherapy.  
[DOI: 10.1007/s00262-017-1985-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00262-017-1985-z)

## Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses

**Stout et al. (2017)** conducted a systematic review of 53 existing systematic reviews (2005-2017), finding that exercise is beneficial before, during, and after cancer treatment across all cancer types. Moderate-to-vigorous intensity yielded superior improvements in physical function.  
[DOI: 10.1016/j.pmrj.2017.07.074](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pmrj.2017.07.074)

**Mishra et al. (2012)** performed a Cochrane systematic review of 56 RCTs (4,826 participants) on exercise during active treatment. They found improvements in HRQoL, physical functioning, role function, social functioning, and reductions in fatigue.  
[DOI: 10.1002/14651858.CD008465.pub2](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD008465.pub2)

**Medeiros Torres et al. (2022)** meta-analyzed 20 RCTs (1,793 participants) on fatigue during adjuvant chemotherapy/radiotherapy for breast cancer. Supervised combined resistance + aerobic training was most effective (SMD = -1.13) for reducing fatigue.  
[DOI: 10.1007/s00520-022-06809-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00520-022-06809-w)

**Malveiro et al. (2023)** reviewed exercise during neoadjuvant treatment, finding promising evidence for improvements in cardiorespiratory fitness, muscle strength, body composition, and quality of life.  
[DOI: 10.1016/j.jsams.2023.08.178](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsams.2023.08.178)

## Clinical Trials

**Min et al. (2024)** published in *JAMA Surgery* an RCT showing that a 1-month tailored exercise program immediately after breast cancer surgery significantly improved shoulder function, with 78.6% of the exercise group fully recovering ROM vs. 21.4% in usual care.  
[DOI: 10.1001/jamasurg.2024.1633](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamasurg.2024.1633)

**Demmelmaier et al. (2021)** conducted the Phys-Can multicenter RCT (577 participants with breast, prostate, or colorectal cancer) comparing exercise intensities. Both high and low-to-moderate intensity were safe and effective for cancer-related fatigue, with small advantages for high intensity in physical fatigue.  
[DOI: 10.1111/sms.13930](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.13930)

**Aydin et al. (2021)** found that 12 weeks of aerobic and stretching exercises significantly improved quality of life and decreased depression in breast cancer survivors who had completed treatment.  
[DOI: 10.31557/APJCP.2021.22.3.725](https://doi.org/10.31557/APJCP.2021.22.3.725)

## Prehabilitation and Cardio-Oncology

**Del Bianco et al. (2024)** systematically reviewed 96 studies on exercise prehabilitation across gynecological, breast, urological, gastrointestinal, and lung cancers, finding it may improve physical fitness and postoperative outcomes.  
[DOI: 10.1016/j.critrevonc.2024.104350](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.critrevonc.2024.104350)

**Tong et al. (2020)** focus on cardiotoxicity, showing exercise therapy improves cardiorespiratory fitness and attenuates the cardiovascular effects of cancer treatments.  
[DOI: 10.1007/s11864-020-00752-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11864-020-00752-w)
