These are all current OpenAI Foundation board members. Here's what I found about each person's publicly stated views on AGI timelines — though notably, several of them have not made detailed public statements on the topic.

**Bret Taylor (Chair)** views AI as genuinely transformational but is notably more measured than many in the AGI community on timelines. He believes the transition will be "probably slower than people think" because not all parts of the economy can absorb intelligence equally — he uses the analogy of drug development, where even superintelligence wouldn't speed up clinical trials. As OpenAI's chairman, he has said that AGI remains a distant goal, though incremental progress in agent capabilities is already producing real results. He defines AGI as a system capable of performing any task a person can do at a computer at equal or better proficiency, with generalization to new domains as the key differentiator.

**Adam D'Angelo** is the most vocal and optimistic of the group. In mid-2024, he predicted AGI would arrive "within five to 15 years," calling it a "very, very important change in the world when we get there." He has also posted publicly that AGI will likely be "the most important event in the history of the world" and that it will happen in our lifetimes. More recently, in late 2025, he expressed surprise at bearishness around AGI, saying progress in reasoning models, code generation, and video generation seemed to be going faster than ever. He has proposed a functional definition: AGI is achieved when AI can do any job done by a typical remote human worker, and believes current models are mainly held back by context rather than intelligence.

**Dr. Sue Desmond-Hellmann** is a physician-scientist and former CEO of the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation who joined the OpenAI board in March 2024. Her public commentary focuses on AI's potential to transform healthcare, diagnostics, and drug development rather than on specific AGI timeline predictions. I could not find any public statements from her offering a specific view on when AGI might arrive. Her presence on the board is generally understood to bring a lens of global health, equity, and responsible innovation.

**Dr. Zico Kolter** is the head of CMU's Machine Learning Department and chairs OpenAI's Safety and Security Committee. He has described himself as having changed his mind about AGI, now believing it might arrive much sooner than he previously expected — a stance described as being "AGI-pilled." However, he is primarily focused on safety risks rather than timeline predictions. He has acknowledged that "very few people, even people working in machine learning deeply, really anticipated the current state we are in, the explosion of capabilities, the explosion of risks that are emerging right now." His concern spans everything from existential risks to near-term harms like mental health impacts, cybersecurity vulnerabilities, and whether models could help create bioweapons.

**Retired Gen. Paul M. Nakasone** was appointed primarily for his cybersecurity expertise as the former head of both NSA and U.S. Cyber Command. He has not made detailed public statements about AGI timelines. His stated motivation for joining was that "OpenAI's dedication to its mission aligns closely with my own values and experience in public service," and he focuses on ensuring AGI is "safe and beneficial." His role is more about securing AI systems on the path to AGI than predicting when it will arrive.

**Adebayo Ogunlesi** is a global infrastructure investor (founder of GIP, now a senior managing director at BlackRock) who joined the board in January 2025. He has not made public statements about AGI timelines. His stated perspective is that "the rapid advancement and development of AI offers a unique opportunity to build a better future" and that infrastructure investment will be key to unlocking AI's potential. His expertise is in the massive capital deployment needed to build AI infrastructure, not in making technical predictions about AGI arrival.

**Nicole Seligman** is a former EVP and General Counsel at Sony Corporation and former president of Sony Corporation of America, with a distinguished legal career before that. She joined the board in March 2024 and serves on the Safety and Security Committee. I found no public statements from her offering specific views on AGI timelines. Her role appears oriented toward governance, legal, and regulatory oversight.

In short, only Taylor, D'Angelo, and Kolter have spoken publicly about AGI timelines in any substantive way — with D'Angelo being the most bullish, Kolter increasingly convinced it's coming sooner than expected, and Taylor cautioning that societal adoption will be slower than technologists predict. The other four members bring domain expertise (healthcare, cybersecurity, infrastructure, legal governance) but have not publicly weighed in on when AGI might arrive.
