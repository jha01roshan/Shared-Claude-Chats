## Human

What is the best way to fit a new tubed motorcycle?  Put one side of the tyre on and fit the tube or put tube in tyre before fitting tyre

---

## Assistant

The best method is **put one bead on the rim first, then fit the tube**. Here's why and how:

## The Correct Technique

1. **Mount one bead of the tyre onto the rim** — get one side fully seated in the rim before touching the tube.

2. **Slightly inflate the tube** — just enough to give it shape (don't over-inflate). This stops it folding or creasing inside the tyre.

3. **Insert the tube into the tyre** — push the valve stem through the rim hole first, then work the tube evenly around inside the tyre.

4. **Work the second bead onto the rim** — using tyre levers if needed, starting opposite the valve and working around both sides toward the valve last.

5. **Check the tube isn't pinched** — before fully inflating, press the tyre away from the rim all the way around both sides to make sure the tube isn't trapped under the bead.

6. **Inflate slowly** — stop partway and check the tyre is seating evenly, then inflate to the correct pressure.

---

## Why NOT to fully assemble tube-in-tyre first?

Putting the tube fully inside the tyre before fitting either bead makes it very easy to **pinch or twist the tube** as you wrestle the tyre onto the rim — it has nowhere to move and folds on itself. This is the most common cause of **instant punctures** after a tyre change.

---

## Key Tips to Avoid Problems

- **Never use tyre levers near the valve** — always finish the last section of bead away from the valve area.
- **Use washing-up liquid or tyre soap** on the beads — makes fitting much easier and reduces the risk of using levers aggressively.
- **Don't use levers carelessly** — most tubes are pinched by levers, not by the tyre itself.
- **Deflate slightly after seating** — let a little air out, check everything is seated, then re-inflate. This lets the bead settle properly.

One bead on first is the method used by professional tyre fitters for a reason — it gives the tube room to sit naturally without stress.

## Assistant

Great tips from real-world experience! Those are genuinely useful techniques:

**Standing on the tyre** — this is a classic pro trick that doesn't get mentioned enough. It pushes the bead down into the well of the rim (the centre drop section), which is slightly smaller in diameter and gives you the slack you need to work the opposite side over the rim edge. Without doing this, you're fighting the tyre unnecessarily.

**Two levers in place before the final push** — completely agree. If you try to do the last tight section one lever at a time, the bead springs back and you lose your progress. Having two levers holding position while you work the bead over with a third (or by hand if you're lucky) is much more controlled and less likely to result in a pinched tube.

**Stiff adventure tyres on non-tubeless rims** — that's a particularly tough combination. Tubeless adventure tyres are built with much stiffer sidewalls and beads by design, and without the tubeless rim's tighter bead seat to help lock things in place, they can be an absolute battle. Good tip to flag that for anyone attempting the same thing — factor in extra time and patience.
