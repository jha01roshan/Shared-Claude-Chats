**The Substitute**

The goat was eating Principal Vance's toupee when Marisol finally admitted she might have made a mistake.

"This is fine," she said, watching through the faculty lounge window as the animal -- a substantial Alpine with a coat like dirty snow -- chewed thoughtfully on what had, until thirty seconds ago, been perched atop the principal's head. "This is totally fine."

"This is not fine," said Raj, her best friend and current co-conspirator in disaster. He was holding a clipboard that said PETTING ZOO COORDINATOR in letters Marisol had carefully forged to match the school district's official font. "Marisol. That goat is eating a man's hair."

"It's not his *real* hair."

"That somehow makes it worse!"

The plan had been simple. Elegant, even. Marisol's older sister Daniela was supposed to give the Career Day presentation about veterinary medicine. Daniela, who was competent and professional and had never accidentally released livestock into Jefferson Middle School. But Daniela had texted at 6 AM: *Food poisoning. Dying. Tell Mom I want the good casket.*

Marisol, thirteen years old and possessing what her mother called "more ambition than sense," had made a decision. She would give the presentation herself. How hard could talking about animals be? She'd had a hamster once. It had lived for almost three months.

The problems began when she arrived at Sunny Acres Farm to pick up the "small, manageable animals for educational purposes" that Daniela had arranged. Mr. Henrickson, who ran the farm and apparently didn't believe in verifying identities, had loaded up the van while Marisol stood frozen in terror, unable to admit she was not, in fact, a twenty-two-year-old veterinary student.

"Is the goat supposed to come?" she'd asked weakly.

"Gustav? Sure, sure. Kids love Gustav. Very docile." Mr. Henrickson had patted the goat's flank with the confidence of someone who had never seen Gustav's true nature.

Now Gustav had somehow escaped the multipurpose room, navigated the hallways, and discovered Principal Vance during what was supposed to be a routine inspection of the Career Day setup. The principal was making a sound Marisol had never heard a human produce before -- a sort of high-pitched keening that suggested his soul was leaving his body.

"We have to fix this," Raj said. "My mom is the vice principal. If she finds out I helped you--"

"She won't find out." Marisol was already moving toward the door. "We just need to get Gustav back to the multipurpose room before anyone realizes he wasn't supposed to leave."

"The multipurpose room where you are also hiding an unauthorized llama!"

"Sparkles is not unauthorized. She's just... unexpectedly included."

The llama had been another surprise from Mr. Henrickson. "Bonus animal," he'd said cheerfully. "No extra charge!"

Marisol burst into the hallway just as Gustav, having apparently decided the toupee was not to his liking, spat it onto the linoleum and began trotting toward the eighth-grade wing. Principal Vance remained frozen in place, one hand raised to his now-exposed bald head, his expression suggesting he was reconsidering every life choice that had led to this moment.

"Principal Vance!" Marisol used her most responsible voice, the one she saved for parent-teacher conferences and police officers. "Don't worry! This is all part of the demonstration!"

"Demonstration?" he repeated faintly.

"About... animal behavior in unfamiliar environments. Very educational. My sister -- I mean, I -- planned this whole thing." She was speed-walking after Gustav, who had paused to investigate a water fountain. "Raj, get the toupee!"

Raj looked at the sodden hairpiece on the floor with the expression of someone being asked to defuse a bomb. "Absolutely not."

Gustav chose that moment to spot something far more interesting than water fountains or toupees: an open door to the school cafeteria, where lunch setup was underway. The goat's ears perked up. His nostrils flared. And then he was moving, faster than Marisol would have thought possible for an animal that size, his hooves clattering on the tile like castanets.

"NO!" Marisol sprinted after him, her sneakers squeaking. "GUSTAV, NO!"

But Gustav had already crossed the threshold into the cafeteria, where he was greeted by the sight of industrial-sized containers of food, the smell of today's mystery meat, and three lunch ladies who would later describe the next five minutes as "like that scene from Jurassic Park, but with more bleating."

Marisol skidded through the cafeteria entrance to find Gustav standing atop a lunch table, surveying his domain like a conquering general. Beneath him, scattered trays and spilled juice boxes created a battlefield of chaos. The lunch ladies had retreated behind the service counter, where they watched with expressions ranging from horror to grudging respect.

"Okay," Marisol said, mostly to herself. "Okay. Think. What would Daniela do?"

Daniela would not have impersonated her sister to give a Career Day presentation. Daniela would not have accepted livestock from a stranger. Daniela would probably be in bed right now, not food-poisoned at all, just avoiding family dinner because she was actually on a date with that dental hygienist she'd been texting.

*Wait.*

That was it. Daniela wasn't sick. She'd bailed on purpose, probably assuming Mom would just cancel the whole thing. Which meant Marisol's carefully forged permission slip, her borrowed veterinary textbook, her entire elaborate scheme to prove she was responsible enough to be trusted with important tasks -- all of it was built on a lie that no one had even asked her to tell.

"Marisol." Raj appeared at her elbow, breathing hard. He was holding the toupee at arm's length like it might explode. "Principal Vance is coming. And he's bringing Vice Principal Santos."

Vice Principal Santos. Raj's mother. Who, unlike Principal Vance, did not have a reputation for being easily confused by creative explanations.

Gustav chose that moment to begin eating a plastic container of what the menu claimed was "Salisbury steak." The container was not opened. This did not appear to concern him.

From somewhere behind her, Marisol heard the multipurpose room door crash open, followed by a sound that could only be described as "llama alarm cry." Sparkles had apparently tired of confinement.

"So," Raj said, with the calm of someone who has passed through panic into a state of transcendent acceptance, "what's the plan?"

Marisol watched Gustav successfully breach the Salisbury steak container and begin consuming its contents with evident satisfaction. She heard Principal Vance's voice in the hallway, rising in pitch. She heard Sparkles' hoofbeats -- *can llamas gallop?* apparently yes they can -- growing louder.

And then she heard something else: the click of heels that could only belong to Vice Principal Santos, accompanied by her mother's voice saying, "Raj told me Daniela was doing a presentation today. I wanted to stop by and--"

Marisol's mom. Here. Now.

The universe, Marisol decided, had a terrible sense of humor.

Gustav locked eyes with her across the cafeteria. In his gaze, she saw what might have been understanding, or possibly just goat thoughts. He swallowed the last of the Salisbury steak, plastic container and all, and let out a burp that echoed off the cafeteria walls like a challenge.

Behind her, footsteps were approaching. Multiple sets. At least three adults who were about to discover that Marisol Rodriguez had spent the morning committing what was probably several different kinds of fraud, all to avoid admitting she wasn't her sister.

Sparkles galloped past the cafeteria door, pursued by what sounded like the entire janitorial staff.

Marisol took a deep breath.

"Raj," she said quietly, "I need you to tell me the truth. On a scale of one to ten, how much trouble am I in?"

Raj looked at her. Looked at Gustav. Looked at the approaching adults. Looked at the toupee in his hands, which he seemed only now to remember he was holding.

"Twelve," he said. "You're at a twelve."

The disappointment in her voice was worse than anger. Marisol felt it like a physical weight.

Marisol's mom materialized beside her like a summoned demon. "We're leaving. Now. Animals are loaded, Raj is going home with his mother, and you and I are going to have a very long conversation about honesty, responsibility, and why I shouldn't ground you until you're thirty."

The drive home was silent except for the occasional bleat from Gustav in the back. Marisol watched the suburbs scroll past--familiar houses, familiar lawns, the same route she'd taken to school every day for three years. Everything looked exactly the same, but she felt fundamentally altered, like she'd traded something essential for something she didn't fully understand yet.

"And did you?"

Marisol thought about the chaos, the improvisations, the ten-minute miracle that had somehow worked. "Kind of?"

Her mom laughed--a short, surprised sound. "That's the most honest thing you've said all day." She stood. "Go clean your room. I'm ordering pizza for dinner. Even disasters deserve pepperoni."

Upstairs, Marisol checked her phone one last time before surrender. A text from Raj: *Coach Brennan just picked Emma Chen as your replacement. Emma can't tell a phylum from a filing cabinet. We're doomed.*

And from Daniela: *Mom told me everything. You're INSANE. Call me.*

Marisol didn't call. She opened her laptop and searched "how to train a mini-pig" instead. The results were sparse and contradictory. One site suggested clicker training. Another recommended "establishing dominance through calm assertion." A third simply said, "Good luck, you'll need it."

Her bedroom door cracked open. Her mom held out the phone. "It's for you. Vice Principal Santos."

Marisol's stomach clenched. She took the phone.

"Miss Rodriguez," Santos said, "I wanted to let you know that Bacon Bits has a small... complication."

"What kind of complication?"

"He's bonded to Gustav. Mr. Henrickson tried to separate them, and Bacon Bits became extremely distressed. Screaming, actually. For twenty minutes." Santos paused. "You'll need to coordinate with Jefferson High for shared animal custody. Gustav is part of their FFA program now."

"Jefferson High? That's where Daniela--"

"Goes to school, yes. The FFA advisor is Ms. Patricia Rodriguez. Your sister."

Marisol closed her eyes. Of course. Of course Daniela had lied about the veterinary school thing. She was still in high school, probably taking some agriculture elective for easy credits, and now Marisol would have to see her multiple times per week to access a pig who'd bonded with a goat who'd eaten their principal's toupee.

"Marisol?" Santos's voice was careful. "This is going to be more complicated than you thought."

"Yeah," Marisol said. "I'm getting that."
