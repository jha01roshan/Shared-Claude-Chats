Henry, this is an important topic with some nuance. There's a distinction between **manufacturer liability immunity** (shielding companies from lawsuits) and **no-fault compensation programs** (government-funded systems that compensate injured individuals without requiring proof of negligence). Many countries have the latter without granting full manufacturer immunity.

**Manufacturer Liability Immunity**

Among the 18 countries studied during COVID-19, only the US and Canada provided direct immunity from liability for healthcare workers administering vaccines during the pandemic. In the US, this comes through two main mechanisms: the 1986 National Childhood Vaccine Injury Act, the 2005 PREP Act, and the Supreme Court's *Bruesewitz v. Wyeth* decision, which together afford vaccine manufacturers almost blanket liability protection.

The **UK** has a more limited form: EU-derived law (adopted by the UK) protects manufacturers from liability when the government supplies their vaccines without full licensing, though manufacturers can still be held liable if their product was defective.

**Germany** offers limited liability under emergency use authorizations, similar to the FDA's approach.

**No-Fault Vaccine Injury Compensation Programs**

A larger group of countries have set up no-fault compensation systems — these don't necessarily grant manufacturers full immunity, but they channel claims away from the courts. As of 2018, 25 jurisdictions worldwide had established no-fault compensation programs, mostly in high-income countries. These include:

**Long-established programs (1960s–1980s):** Germany (1961), France, UK, Denmark, Japan, Switzerland, New Zealand, Sweden, Finland, and the US.

**More recent additions:** Canada and Australia first established no-fault systems in 2021 post-COVID. Singapore launched a Vaccine Injury Financial Assistance Programme in January 2021 specifically for COVID-19, though it lacks overarching legislation. Taiwan and South Korea also operate compensation programs, with Taiwan funding its program through a levy of about $0.05 per vaccine dose.

Other countries with programs include Italy, Slovenia, China, Norway, Hungary, and — notably among lower-income countries — Nepal and Vietnam, which were the first low- and lower-middle-income countries to institute such programs.

**International mechanism:** The COVAX No-Fault Compensation Program was created as the world's first international vaccine injury compensation mechanism, covering 92 low- and middle-income countries for COVID-19 vaccines distributed through COVAX.

**Approval rates vary enormously:** Japan has the highest approval rate at about 74%, while the US (3%) and UK (2.6%) have the lowest among the 14 jurisdictions studied.

So the bottom line: full manufacturer immunity like the US has is quite rare — really just the US and (during COVID) Canada. But around 25 countries have no-fault compensation programs that serve as an alternative pathway, and many more were covered internationally through COVAX during the pandemic.
