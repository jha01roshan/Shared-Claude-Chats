## Assistant

A **DXA scan** (Dual-Energy X-ray Absorptiometry) primarily scans for:

**Bone Health**
- **Bone mineral density (BMD)** — the main purpose, used to diagnose osteoporosis and osteopenia
- Fracture risk assessment
- Monitoring response to osteoporosis treatment

**Body Composition**
- **Fat mass vs. lean mass** distribution throughout the body
- Visceral fat (fat around internal organs)
- Muscle mass — useful for detecting sarcopenia (muscle loss with aging)

**Other Uses**
- Tracking changes in body composition over time (useful in research, athletics, and clinical settings)
- Assessing bone health in people on long-term corticosteroids, with malabsorption conditions, or post-menopause

The scan works by passing two low-dose X-ray beams at different energy levels through the body. Different tissues (bone, fat, muscle) absorb the beams differently, allowing precise measurement.

It's particularly relevant for your research interests — vitamin D deficiency is a major driver of low BMD, and DXA is the gold-standard tool for measuring the bone outcomes that vitamin D supplementation trials often track.
