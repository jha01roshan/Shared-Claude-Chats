## Assistant

Applying ethnomethodology to protocol studies opens up fascinating possibilities for understanding how thinking-aloud is accomplished as a social and methodological practice. Here are some key directions you might explore:

**Focus on the Social Organization of Protocol Production**
Rather than treating protocols as transparent windows into cognition, examine how participants and researchers jointly accomplish the activity of "thinking aloud." Look at how participants learn to produce accountable descriptions of their thinking, and how they orient to the researcher's presence and expectations.

**Analyze the Indexical Properties of Protocol Utterances**
Ethnomethodology emphasizes how meaning depends on context. Study how protocol statements gain their sense through their sequential placement, the specific task context, and participants' ongoing interpretive work. What makes a protocol utterance recognizably about "thinking" rather than just talking?

**Examine Reflexivity in Verbal Protocols**
Explore the reflexive relationship between thinking and talking about thinking. How does the act of verbalizing change the very cognitive processes being studied? This could reveal the performative dimensions of protocol data.

**Study Membership Categorization in Protocols**
Analyze how participants invoke categories like "expert," "novice," or "problem-solver" in their protocols, and how these identity categories shape what counts as appropriate thinking-aloud behavior.

**Investigate Repair and Trouble in Protocol Interaction**
Look at moments when the protocol "breaks down" - when participants struggle to verbalize, when researchers intervene, or when the thinking-aloud becomes problematic. These moments can reveal the normally invisible work of maintaining the protocol as a viable research method.

Would you like to explore any of these directions more deeply, or discuss specific methodological approaches for this kind of analysis?
