## ⚡ Electric Lawn Mowers — Global Trends

**Market Size & Growth**

The global electric lawn mower market was valued at around USD $9–17 billion in 2024 (estimates vary by research firm) and is projected to grow at a CAGR of roughly 5–6% through 2033. The wide range in valuations reflects how analysts define "electric" — some include robotic and ride-on commercial mowers.

**Regional Leaders**

North America holds the largest market share, driven by strong demand in the US and Canada. Europe follows closely, accounting for around 30% of the market, driven by eco-conscious consumers and strict government emissions rules. Asia-Pacific, though currently smaller at around 20%, is growing fastest. The Asia-Pacific region is forecast to record the fastest regional growth at a 16.8% CAGR from 2026 to 2031.

**Key Drivers**
A California Air Resources Board report found that small off-road engines like lawn mowers and leaf blowers collectively contribute more emissions than 14.4 million passenger cars in that state alone — a striking fact that has accelerated regulatory pressure on gas-powered garden equipment globally.

Walk-behind cordless mowers held 54.3% of market share in 2025. Robotic and autonomous platforms are poised to surge at a 17.1% CAGR through 2031, driven by rising labour costs and acceptance of unattended mowing.

**Do They Use Lithium Batteries?**

Yes, overwhelmingly. Battery technology advances, especially involving lithium-ion, are critical in extending run times and reducing charging periods. While older models used nickel-cadmium (Ni-Cd) or nickel-metal hydride (Ni-MH) chemistries, lithium-ion has become the dominant standard due to its higher energy density, lighter weight, and longer cycle life. As battery pack prices have fallen — with Chinese lithium-iron phosphate (LFP) cells becoming significantly cheaper by early 2024 — this has eliminated the cost premium that once restricted cordless mowers to a niche market.

## 🏍️ Electric Motorcycles in Asia — Explosive Growth

**Global Picture**

Two- and three-wheelers remain the most electrified road transport segment globally, with more than 9% of the global fleet now electric. Global electric model sales reached around 10 million in 2024, with a sales share of approximately 15%.

**China — The Dominant Force**

China holds the highest electric two-wheeler sales share globally at 54.8% in 2025. China produces over 40 million electric two-wheelers per year, and major cities like Shanghai and Shenzhen are now nearly fully electric in two-wheeler usage. Interestingly, China's electric motorcycle sales have actually been on a slight downward trend since 2021 domestically, as the market matures, driving Chinese manufacturers to aggressively expand abroad.

**India — Rapid Acceleration**

India had an electric two-wheeler sales share of 6.5% in 2025, and is far ahead of all other markets besides China in absolute electric two-wheeler sales. Two-wheeler electrification is poised to play an important role in India's broader EV industry, as two-wheelers accounted for 77% of vehicle sales and production in fiscal year 2024–25. India's central government offers purchase subsidies of up to INR 5,000/kWh for two-wheelers fitted with lithium-ion batteries, with a total budget of USD 1.3 billion planned to support the rollout of about 2.5 million electric two-wheelers.

**Vietnam — The Star Performer**

Vietnam has emerged as the world's most dramatic electric motorcycle growth story. Sales of electric motorcycles in Vietnam reached 209,000 units in just the first half of 2025, a 99.2% year-on-year surge, making it the world's third-largest electric motorcycle market behind China and India.

Vietnam ranked second globally in electric two-wheeler sales share at 21.7% in 2025 — a dramatic jump from around 10% in 2024. This surge reflects market reaction to plans in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City to implement low-emission zones that could ban or restrict gasoline two-wheelers from city centres.

Sales of full-sized electric bike models surged 197% in the first eight months of 2025. Local manufacturer VinFast saw sales more than quadruple and is planning to roll out 150,000 battery-swapping stations across Vietnam within three years.

**Do Asian Electric Motorcycles Use Lithium Batteries?**

The short answer is: increasingly yes, but lead-acid is still common in lower-end models. Unlike cars, many electric two-wheeler models do not rely on extensive charging infrastructure, as removable batteries allow users to charge at home using standard sockets — a significant advantage in markets with limited public charging. The Indian government specifically incentivises lithium-ion adoption, while battery-swapping networks in Vietnam and China are accelerating the shift to lithium chemistries by making the upfront battery cost less of a barrier.

## 🌫️ Are Electric Motorbikes Improving Air Quality in Asian Cities?

This is perhaps the most significant dimension of the whole trend, and the answer is: **they are beginning to, but the transition is still in early stages for most cities.**

**Vietnam — Policy-Driven Urgency**

Hanoi's air quality often ranks among the world's worst, with AQI hitting "very poor" levels (115–165) in late 2024 and early 2025. The roughly 6.9 million motorcycles in Hanoi — the vast majority petrol-powered — are a major contributor, pumping out nitrogen dioxide and fine particulate matter. Gasoline-powered models contribute to as much as 60% of the city's air pollution, with nearly 70% of those motorcycles failing to meet current emission standards.

From the beginning of 2025 to mid-July 2025, Hanoi recorded good air quality on only 34% of days.

In response, Prime Minister Pham Minh Chinh signed Directive 20/CT-TTg in July 2025, mandating a phased restriction and eventual prohibition of gasoline motorcycles in Hanoi's central urban areas. Phase 1 begins July 2026, covering the Ring Road 1 districts, expanding further in 2028 and 2030. Vietnam aims to phase out all gas-powered two-wheelers nationally by 2045.

Ho Chi Minh City has set a target to transition 400,000 ride-hailing gasoline motorcycles to electric by 2029, aiming for a 100% reduction in air pollution from that vehicle category. The focus on high-mileage commercial riders is smart: each ride-hailing driver travels 80–120 km daily — three to four times more than ordinary citizens — making conversion of this group particularly impactful for emission reduction.

**India — Ambition vs. Reality**

Six of the world's ten most polluted cities are in India, and New Delhi has the lowest air quality of any capital city globally. Air pollution kills over 1.2 million people in India every year, with transportation being one of the biggest contributing factors.

Delhi has launched an ambitious 2025 Air Pollution Mitigation Plan. Delhi plans to ban new internal combustion engine two-wheeler sales by 2027 and is rolling out around 5,000 electric buses and 2,300 electric auto-rickshaws. The city aims to completely switch to electric buses for public transport by 2027. In Bangalore and Delhi, Zypp Electric has partnered with operators to deploy 30,000 high-speed electric scooters for food delivery and e-commerce, with EVs reducing direct costs by approximately 40% compared to petrol bikes — giving commercial riders a strong financial incentive to switch.

**The Caveats**

While the direction is clearly positive, experts note important nuances:

- **Grid electricity sources matter.** If the electricity powering these bikes comes from coal plants (still dominant in India and parts of Southeast Asia), the pollution is partly shifted rather than eliminated — though electric bikes still tend to produce significantly less total pollution even on coal-heavy grids.
- **Infrastructure gaps** remain a major hurdle, especially outside city centres in both Vietnam and India.
- Experts warn of potential "gas bike dumping" in rural areas, where older petrol vehicles may be sold or abandoned rather than retired, simply shifting pollution from urban to rural regions.
- Delhi's own plan acknowledges that electrification alone won't solve the city's transport crisis — transboundary pollution from crop burning, industry, and neighbouring states plays a significant role that city-level EV policy cannot address.

**The Bottom Line**

The transition to electric motorcycles in Asia is one of the most consequential environmental trends currently underway. Electrification of the two-wheeler fleet will bring great benefits in decarbonising road transport and reducing consumer fuel costs. Electric two-wheelers emit zero tailpipe emissions, reducing harmful pollution that riders and pedestrians are most directly exposed to. In cities like Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City, Delhi and Bangalore, where motorcycles and scooters are the dominant form of transport and air quality is chronically poor, the mass electrification of this fleet — if the electricity grid also continues to clean up — represents one of the single most impactful levers available to improve urban air quality and public health.
