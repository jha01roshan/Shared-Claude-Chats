Good data to work from. Here are five independently active voices worth knowing, each with a distinct angle:

---

**1. Robin Good**
The most dedicated practitioner-theorist of digital curation as a professional skill. He helps expert consultants become trusted, high-authority go-to guides by teaching them how to become great curators, with a focus on depth, authenticity, information design, and tools. He runs two active Substack publications: *TRUST-able* and *Curation Monetized*, which documents real-world examples of online projects that profit by organizing already-published information. His is probably the most practitioner-facing voice on curation specifically — and the most relevant to your positioning work.

---

**2. Harold Jarche**
The originator of the **Seek–Sense–Share** framework, which remains the most widely cited model for personal knowledge mastery (PKM) among professionals and consultants. He frames curation as fundamentally about sense-making: filtering signal from noise, validating reliability, synthesizing patterns, and presenting information in understandable form. His blog at jarche.com is sparse but consistent, aimed squarely at networked knowledge workers and organizational consultants.

---

**3. Tiago Forte**
Best known for *Building a Second Brain* and the PARA organizational system. His book continues to sell around 10,000 copies monthly, generating ongoing demand for coaching and consulting on Personal Knowledge Management from individuals to large companies. He's less "curation theory" and more applied PKM systems for knowledge entrepreneurs — but his frameworks (CODE, PARA) are effectively curation infrastructure for expert practitioners.

---

**4. Anne-Laure Le Cunff**
Founder of *Ness Labs*, writing at the intersection of neuroscience, learning, and knowledge work. Her audience is explicitly knowledge entrepreneurs, researchers, and consultants trying to think and learn better at scale. She addresses curation through the lens of "mindful productivity" — what to capture, how to connect ideas, and how to avoid cognitive overload. Less tool-centric than Forte, more epistemically rigorous than most in this space.

---

**5. Maggie Appleton**
A more niche but influential voice in the **digital gardening** movement — the idea of cultivating a networked, public knowledge base over time rather than publishing finished content. She writes and speaks primarily for designers, developers, and knowledge-system builders. Her concept of "epistemic disclosure" (showing what you know vs. speculate) is genuinely useful for consultants building thought authority. Active on her site maggieappleton.com and in the tools-for-thought community.

---

**The key distinction across these five:** Robin Good and Harold Jarche write *about* curation as a professional discipline. Forte and Le Cunff write *through* curation as knowledge practitioners. Appleton writes about the *architecture* of curated knowledge systems. Depending on what angle you're developing, these are quite different models to learn from.
