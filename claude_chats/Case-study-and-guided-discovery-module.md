## DISCOVERY POINT PLAN

```
MODULE 1 — Socratic Clinical Reasoning
  DP-1.1  "This patient had severe jaundice, total failure to tolerate feeds
            except salty broth, abdominal oedema, and a 2-hour sleep cycle from
            birth. If you had no genetic data, what would your working diagnosis
            be at age 1 — and what is the single most informative test?"
  DP-1.2  "She lost 10–15 lbs within 2 days of fasting repeatedly across her
            life. What fluid compartment does that water come from — and what
            mechanism of oedema is consistent with that rapid flux?"
  DP-1.3  "Stimulants cause her to sleep; coffee makes her tired. How does
            the AMPD1 deficiency + adenosine dysregulation model explain this?
            What alternative explanation exists?"
  DP-1.4  "She broke her leg dancing and walked on it for 2 weeks without
            registering pain. Is this peripheral neuropathy, central pain
            processing difference, or autism-spectrum sensory difference —
            and how would you distinguish them?"
  DP-1.5  "Her left side is predominantly affected — numbness, face droop,
            weakness, falling to the left. What single neurological lesion
            localises to all of these, and has it ever been imaged during
            an acute episode?"

MODULE 12 — Differential Diagnosis Deepdive
  DP-12.1 "Rank these five diagnoses by pre-test probability given the full
            42-year phenotype: GSD type III, GSD type IX, Behçet's disease,
            hereditary angioedema (non-HAE type), WNK1 channelopathy. Which
            one unifies the most findings — and which findings does it fail
            to explain?"
  DP-12.2 "The oedema worsens with smoke, exercise, emotional stress, and
            eating, but diuretics make it worse, not better. What type of
            oedema is definitionally excluded by diuretic failure? What types
            remain?"
  DP-12.3 "She has VUS genes for GSD III and GSD IX. How much clinical weight
            can you give a VUS — and what one investigation (liver biopsy,
            enzyme assay, or repeat genetic sequencing) would convert this
            from hypothesis to diagnosis?"
  DP-12.4 "PCOS in all GSD girls under 4 years is cited in the case. She
            began shaving at age 3. How strong is this as a discriminating
            feature for GSD, and does any other diagnosis on your list explain
            precocious androgen excess in a 3-year-old?"

MODULE 28 — Diagnostic Time-Out
  DP-28.1 "For over 20 years, all clinical attention was directed at G6PD +
            AMPD1 as the primary diagnoses. Name three clinical findings that
            neither G6PD nor AMPD1 adequately explains — and ask what
            diagnosis does explain all three."
  DP-28.2 "The patient herself arrived at the GSD hypothesis while waiting
            for a rheumatologist to clear her liver before prescribing an
            immunosuppressant. This is self-diagnosis from VUS data and
            patient-group pattern matching. What is the epistemic status of
            this hypothesis — and what would falsify it?"
  DP-28.3 "Behçet's was confirmed by a doctor in 2020. How does Behçet's
            interact with GSD as a co-diagnosis — do they share pathways,
            or are they independent parallel diagnoses?"

MODULE 31 — First-Principles Pathophysiology Mapping
  DP-31.1 "Draw (or describe) the NADPH generation pathway from glucose via
            G6PD → PPP → NADPH → glutathione reductase → GSH. Now mark
            where G6PD deficiency creates a bottleneck. Then add AMPD1
            deficiency to the purine nucleotide cycle. Where do the two
            deficiencies converge to amplify oxidative stress in muscle?"
  DP-31.2 "WNK1 regulates potassium-chloride cotransporters and is linked
            to anhydrosis and ion transport. She craves salt, cannot sweat,
            and has POTS. How does WNK1 dysfunction mechanistically connect
            all three of these features?"
  DP-31.3 "In GSD type III, debranching enzyme is absent. Glycogen cannot
            be fully mobilised during fasting. How does this create
            hypoglycaemia that manifests as nocturnal tachycardia — and
            why did the racing heart at night resolve on a ketogenic diet?"

MODULE 38 — Poly-Crisis & Cascading Failure Simulator
  DP-38.1 "Colchicine was prescribed for Behçet's. In G6PD-deficient patients,
            colchicine is not strictly contraindicated, but oxidative stress
            from any illness can trigger haemolysis. Map the cascade: acute
            Behçet's flare → inflammation → oxidative stress → G6PD-mediated
            haemolysis → renal failure. At which point is the cascade
            interceptable?"
  DP-38.2 "She had cola urine after the 100-mile hike, then was admitted
            with simultaneous pneumonia and kidney infection. What is the
            likely shared mechanism — and what would have happened if she
            had received a sulfa antibiotic for the kidney infection?"
  DP-38.3 "She woke from anaesthesia twice during surgery. What genetic or
            metabolic explanations exist for this — and what pre-anaesthetic
            workup should be mandatory for her now?"

MODULE 13 — Medication Reconciliation
  DP-13.1 "Ribose is being used to bypass the AMP re-synthesis block of AMPD1
            deficiency. What is the evidence base for ribose in AMPD1 — and
            what is the risk of ribose-driven glycolytic flux in a patient
            who may also have GSD (impaired glycogen mobilisation)?"
  DP-13.2 "NAC increases glutathione. L-serine provides substrate for glycine
            and serine-dependent GABA-ergic sleep pathways. Cimetidine reduces
            androgens and histamine. Nattokinase is a fibrinolytic. In a
            patient with VWF association and possible raised ICP, what is the
            bleeding risk of concurrent nattokinase?"
  DP-13.3 "She reacted severely to sulfa drugs as an infant, to antimalarials
            at 32, and to baby aspirin (made her 'really sick'). What is the
            common pharmacological thread — and how does this create a
            restricted antibiotic formulary for her future care?"

MODULE 26 — Bias Auditing
  DP-26.1 "When she presented as a child with weight fluctuation and abdominal
            swelling, her parents were accused of abuse and lost custody. Name
            the cognitive and systemic biases that led to that conclusion
            instead of a metabolic diagnosis."
  DP-26.2 "She was diagnosed with anorexia at age 15 and tube-fed. The refusal
            to eat was actually food-triggered oedema and pain. What bias did
            the clinicians commit — and what cue was available in the history
            that they dismissed?"
  DP-26.3 "She now has a strong belief that GSD explains everything. What
            is the risk of confirmation bias in patient-led hypothesis
            testing using patient support groups as validation — and how
            would you design an objective test?"

MODULE 33 — "Why Now?" Precipitant Hunter
  DP-33.1 "She made a large batch of multi-bean soup including fava beans
            and kept eating it over several days while feeling progressively
            sicker. Map the mechanism: fava beans → vicine/convicine → oxidative
            stress → G6PD haemolysis → ? → neurological deterioration and
            ascites. Is G6PD alone sufficient to explain the neurological
            cascade, or is a second mechanism required?"
  DP-33.2 "Driving through a wildfire caused severe swelling within hours.
            What is the pathophysiological mechanism connecting smoke
            inhalation to oedema in a G6PD + WNK1 + Behçet's patient —
            and what single mediator is most likely the final common pathway?"
  DP-33.3 "Her oedema and migraines have a prodrome: scalp inflammation,
            irrational anxiety/anger, heart-rate jump at night. What do
            these have in common as a biochemical signal — and what does
            that suggest about timing of any intervention?"

MODULE 42 — Clinical Pre-Mortem
  DP-42.1 "Imagine it is 18 months from now and this patient has been
            admitted in haemolytic crisis. Trace the three most plausible
            paths that lead there from her current medication and
            diagnosis list."
  DP-42.2 "She has unresolved left-sided neurological events (aphasia,
            hemiplegia, vision loss) that resolved but were never imaged
            acutely. What is the risk of a future, irreversible event —
            and what one investigation, if done proactively, would best
            stratify that risk?"
  DP-42.3 "Her father died at 52 looking decades older on autopsy; her
            grandfather had early death; her father had a pacemaker before
            40. She has GSD VUS genes — GSD can cause cardiomyopathy.
            Has her cardiac status ever been formally evaluated, and what
            is the pre-mortem plan if it has not?"
```

## LEARNER ROUTING NOTE

This is one of the most instructionally rich cases in the VibeRounds corpus — a 42-year longitudinal record of a patient whose disease was repeatedly misframed because no one mapped the whole picture at once. The highest-yield starting point is **Modules 1 and 12** run together: build your differential from first principles before you look at the genetic labels, because the labels have already anchored multiple clinicians in the wrong place.

From there, **Module 31** (pathophysiology mapping) is essential — this case only makes sense if you can draw the NADPH → oxidative stress → ion transport → glycogen axis mechanistically. **Module 28** (Diagnostic Time-Out) is urgent because a new anchor (GSD) is forming right now, and it needs scrutiny before it becomes as entrenched as the last one.

**Module 13** (Medication Reconciliation) warrants a dedicated session before any management discussion — the colchicine/G6PD interaction alone is a patient-safety issue, and the supplement list is pharmacologically complex.

If time is short: prioritise **Modules 0 → 1 → 12 → 31 → 28 → 13** in that order. Sessions 2 and 3 deepen what you find. If you want to take this toward publication, **Module 9** is waiting — the dual AMPD1+G6PD+GSD+Behçet's combination in one patient with a 42-year documented record may be unique in the literature.
