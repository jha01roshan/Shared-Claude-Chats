The bylaws are actually pretty light on prescribing specific agenda items for monthly meetings. Here's what they require:

**Scheduling & Location**
Monthly meetings are held January through November (the December meeting is replaced by the Annual Meeting for elections). All meetings must be within Omaha unless the board approves otherwise.

**Quorum Requirement**
A quorum must be verified before any business can be conducted — at least 10% of total voting membership including at least two board members.

**Business Conducted at Monthly Meetings**
The bylaws don't spell out a detailed agenda, but several things are implicitly required or expected:

- **New member introductions and votes** — applicants must be introduced and approved by simple majority vote at a general membership meeting.
- **Treasurer's monthly financial report** — the Treasurer is required to provide the board and membership with a monthly report of all transactions.
- **Programs** — the Program Committee (chaired by the Vice President) is responsible for providing a program at every regular meeting.
- **New member announcements** — the Membership Committee chairperson must announce all new members at regular meetings.
- **General club business** — motions, votes, and regular operations are conducted under Robert's Rules of Order Newly Revised (Article VII).

So in short, every monthly meeting needs a quorum, a treasurer's report, a program, and any pending membership applications handled. Beyond that, the bylaws leave the agenda structure to the officers and Robert's Rules.

Great practical question! The bylaws reference **Robert's Rules of Order Newly Revised (RONR)**, which actually has built-in mechanisms designed for exactly this kind of efficiency. Here's how to run a tight 30-minute business meeting:

---

**The Key Tool: A Standard Order of Business**

RONR prescribes a default order of business, but — crucially — the club can adopt a **standing rule** specifying a condensed agenda and time limits. Since Article VII Section 2 already gives the board authority to establish standing rules, this is the proper mechanism to formalize a 30-minute business meeting structure.

---

**A Suggested 30-Minute Agenda**

| Time | Item | Notes |
|------|------|-------|
| 7:00 | Call to Order | President gavels the meeting open |
| 7:01 | Establish Quorum | Secretary confirms 10% voting members + 2 board members present |
| 7:02 | Approval of Minutes | Members can move to **dispense with reading** and approve as distributed (saves 3-4 minutes) |
| 7:04 | Treasurer's Report | Filed for audit rather than voted on — takes 2 minutes |
| 7:06 | Committee Reports | Only committees with **action items or announcements** report; others file written reports |
| 7:12 | New Member Introductions & Votes | Brief intro, motion, vote — roughly 2 minutes per applicant |
| 7:18 | Old Business | Only items already on the agenda; table anything needing discussion |
| 7:22 | New Business | Limit to items that can be resolved quickly; refer complex items to the board |
| 7:28 | Announcements | Brief, non-debatable |
| 7:30 | Recess / Program Begins | President recesses (not adjourns) the meeting |

---

**Specific RONR Techniques to Keep Things Moving**

**Dispense with reading the minutes.** A member moves to "dispense with the reading of the minutes and approve them as distributed" — if copies were emailed in advance, this is entirely proper and saves several minutes.

**Treasurer's report is received, not voted on.** The treasurer presents the report; it's "received and filed" rather than debated. A motion isn't needed unless there's a discrepancy.

**Consent agenda.** The president can group routine, non-controversial items (standard committee reports, routine approvals) into a single consent agenda item. One vote approves everything on it. Any member can pull an item off the consent agenda for separate discussion.

**Written committee reports.** Standing rules can require committees to submit written reports in advance. At the meeting, the chair simply asks "Are there any additions or corrections to the committee reports distributed?" This can collapse 15 minutes of verbal reporting into 60 seconds.

**Time limits on debate.** A standing rule can limit each member to 2 minutes of debate per item, with no more than one round of debate before a vote. This is perfectly proper under RONR.

**Table or refer complex items.** Anything that sparks significant debate gets moved to "refer to the board" or "table to next meeting." The business meeting isn't the place to resolve complex issues — that's what board meetings (held quarterly per Article V Section 5) are for.

**Recess, don't adjourn.** At 7:30 the president declares a recess and the program begins. After the program, the meeting is called back to order and formally adjourned. This keeps everything within one meeting for parliamentary purposes.

---

**The Standing Rule to Adopt**

To make this official, the board should adopt a standing rule along these lines:

> *"Regular membership meetings shall follow a condensed order of business with the goal of completing all business by 7:30 PM. Committee reports shall be submitted in writing in advance and will be received without verbal presentation unless a committee has a specific action item. Debate on any single item shall be limited to two minutes per member. Items requiring extended discussion shall be referred to the board or tabled to the next meeting. The president shall declare a recess at 7:30 PM for the evening program."*

This standing rule is adopted by a simple majority vote at a membership meeting and can be amended the same way — no bylaw change required.
