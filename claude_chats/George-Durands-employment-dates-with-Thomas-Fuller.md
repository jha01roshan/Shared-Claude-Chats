Yes — digging into other sources (Archeion, the Biographical Dictionary of Architects in Canada, and a related entry on one of his apprentices) turns up some things not in the Wikipedia summary:

**Early training before Robinson**
His father, James Durand, ran a building and contracting business in London, Ontario. Noticing his son's artistic talent, James arranged in 1864 for young George to enroll at the school of sculptor and drawing teacher J.R. Peel — a step that predates his later apprenticeship under William Robinson.

**His own apprentices**
Durand trained apprentices of his own, and at least one went on to a notable career: Herbert Edward Matthews became a pupil in Durand's office, qualifying as a Provincial Land Surveyor in 1885. After Durand's death in December 1889, Matthews moved to Toronto, worked under Darling & Curry, then went to New York to work with Charles C. Haight before returning to London, Ontario, and eventually building a substantial career in Winnipeg as a Dominion government architect.

**Freemasonry**
Durand was active in Masonic circles — he was part of St. John's Lodge 209a around 1876–1877, and later led Tuscan Lodge 195 in 1886.

**Toronto City Hall competition specifics**
Beyond just being a finalist, the record shows the financial details: in the first round (1885) Durand's "Provençal Romanesque" design was one of seven finalists and earned him $150. In the 1886 revised competition, submitting under the pseudonym "LEX," he placed second among 13 competitors and won $500. Edward J. Lennox ultimately won.

**Family/political background**
His father James was also a city councillor and ran (unsuccessfully) as a Liberal candidate in the 1867 Ontario general election.

**Granular commission record**
The Dictionary of Architects in Canada entry has an extensive project-by-project list with exact newspaper citations (Free Press, London Advertiser, Catholic Record, etc.) for dozens of houses, churches, and commercial buildings — far more granular than what's summarized in Wikipedia, down to specific street addresses and client names (e.g., houses for John Christie, the Misses Kennedy, John McClary's "Beacon Lodge," etc.).
