Short answer: of your twelve, only two have actual human data in glaucoma or IOP — magnesium and omega-3 — and magnesium is the one with the strongest "hint" because it has repeated (if small) trials showing functional visual-field improvement, not just pressure change.

Here's how they tier out for glaucoma specifically:

**Tier 1 — human evidence**

Magnesium is the standout. A small RCT found 300 mg/day oral magnesium citrate for one month improved visual field mean deviation and pattern standard deviation without changing ocular blood flow, and an earlier study reported that after four weeks both visual field and peripheral blood flow improved. Mechanistically it fits glaucoma unusually well: it improves blood flow by modifying endothelial function via endothelin-1 and nitric oxide, and is neuroprotective by blocking NMDA-related calcium influx and inhibiting glutamate release. That dual hit — vascular dysregulation *and* excitotoxicity — is exactly the normal-tension glaucoma phenotype, which is why it keeps showing up in the Flammer-syndrome literature too.

Omega-3 is the other one with human IOP data. A pooled analysis of two double-masked RCTs (n=105) found roughly an 8% IOP reduction after three months versus a slight increase in the placebo group, plus a herring-caviar-oil pilot in IOP-controlled POAG suggesting neuroprotection. Caveat: the strong IOP RCT was in *normotensive* adults, not glaucoma patients.

**Tier 2 — strong preclinical, thin human**

Resveratrol has the deepest animal literature — a meta-analysis of 30 preclinical studies found it increased RGC survival and slowed retinal thinning, and topical trans-resveratrol reduced IOP up to 25% in steroid-induced ocular hypertension via adenosine A₁ agonism — but human clinical trials remain limited. Curcumin is similar: promising RGC-protection preclinically, with bioavailability the limiting factor, addressed by nanocarrier eye-drop formulations.

**Tier 3 — mechanistic only**

Quercetin, zinc, boron, ginger, progesterone, sulforaphane, butyrate, and berberine have essentially no glaucoma-specific human or even robust preclinical RGC data. Plausible on oxidative-stress/inflammation grounds, but nothing I'd rank.

**What this does NOT show** — worth flagging in your usual style:

- None of these has an RCT demonstrating slowed *progression* (RGC/visual-field loss over years). Magnesium's trials are ~30 patients and one month; that's a signal, not proof.
- The glaucoma benefit for the two Tier-1 compounds does *not* clearly run through the VDR. Magnesium's effect is calcium-channel/NMDA/endothelial; omega-3's is docosanoid/anti-inflammatory/vascular. So if you're building a VDR-activation narrative, glaucoma is a case where the best-supported members of your list help via *non*-VDR mechanisms. The honest framing is "VDR-activating supplements that also happen to have glaucoma evidence," not "help glaucoma *because* they activate VDR."
- The compound with the best recent human neuroprotection data in glaucoma isn't on your list at all — nicotinamide (B3), which has a crossover RCT showing improved inner-retinal function. Adjacent to your interests if you're doing a glaucoma page.

If I had to give one actionable line: magnesium (as citrate or glycinate, ~300 mg/day) is the best-hinted single agent, with omega-3 as a reasonable co-intervention on IOP grounds — and both belong in a "reduces vascular dysregulation / excitotoxicity" bucket rather than a VDR bucket.

For glaucoma specifically, the field has shifted hard toward *neuroprotection* (protecting retinal ganglion cells independent of pressure), because a large fraction of patients keep losing vision despite well-controlled IOP. That reframing changes which supplements matter. Here's the current landscape by evidence tier:

**Tier 1 — human RCT evidence, now with professional-society attention**

Nicotinamide (vitamin B3) + pyruvate is the clear front-runner. The phase 2 RCT (32 open-angle glaucoma patients) found that 3 g/day nicotinamide plus 3 g/day calcium pyruvate for nine weeks improved a median of 8 visual-field test locations versus placebo, and a separate 12-week Australian crossover RCT (57 patients) at 3 g/day nicotinamide improved inner-retinal function on electroretinography. This got enough traction that the American Glaucoma Society and AAO issued a joint position statement in 2025, and a phase 3 trial (Columbia/Stanford, 21 months, 3 g/day NAM + 1 g/day pyruvate) is running. This is as close to "endorsed adjunct" as the supplement world gets in glaucoma.

Citicoline is the other Tier 1 entry, and it's already marketed as a glaucoma adjunct in parts of Europe. A randomized trial (n=73) found DHA + citicoline significantly improved visual-field mean defect and the VF slope over three months — notably, citicoline paired with DHA outperformed either alone. There's also an international multicenter crossover RCT on quality of life.

**Tier 2 — human RCTs, smaller or phenotype-specific**

Omega-3 (DHA/EPA): ~8% IOP reduction in a normotensive RCT, plus a POAG neuroprotection pilot; works best as the DHA partner to citicoline rather than solo.

Magnesium: small RCTs showing visual-field improvement, via a vascular (endothelin-1/NO) plus anti-excitotoxic (NMDA blockade) mechanism — best suited to the normal-tension / Flammer-syndrome vasospastic phenotype.

Ginkgo biloba: a randomized crossover trial in Chinese normal-tension glaucoma patients showed visual-field improvement, consistent with its ocular-blood-flow effect. Again, an NTG/vascular-phenotype agent.

**Tier 3 — strong preclinical, thin human**

Resveratrol (30-study preclinical meta-analysis, SIRT1-mediated RGC survival; topical lowered IOP in animals), curcumin (RGC protection preclinically, bioavailability-limited, nanocarrier eye-drops in development), and emerging players like saffron/crocin, CoQ10, and taurine.

**If you want the one-line practical hierarchy:** nicotinamide + pyruvate is the best-evidenced and most actively validated; citicoline + DHA is the most established in actual clinical practice; magnesium and ginkgo are the picks for the normal-tension/vascular phenotype; omega-3 is a sensible general adjunct.

**What this does NOT show** — the honest caveats for your curation:

- Every Tier 1/2 endpoint is a *functional or structural research measure* (visual-field locations, ERG, RNFL) over weeks to months. None has yet proven it slows long-term progression or produces patient-meaningful vision recovery — that's exactly what the ongoing phase 3 nicotinamide trials are meant to answer.
- Nicotinamide at 3 g/day has produced documented drug-induced liver injury cases in these trials (including one in an underweight patient). At gram-level dosing this is a real monitoring issue, not a trivial vitamin — worth flagging prominently on any page.
- These are all *adjuncts to* IOP-lowering, not replacements. The entire premise is "additional benefit on top of controlled pressure."
- The IOP-lowering evidence (omega-3, magnesium) and the neuroprotection evidence (nicotinamide, citicoline) are largely separate mechanisms — a page that conflates "lowers pressure" with "protects the nerve" would be miscategorizing them.

One curation note for you: the strongest-evidenced agents here (nicotinamide, citicoline, pyruvate) are all NAD⁺/mitochondrial-bioenergetic or membrane-synthesis plays, not VDR-axis compounds — so this is a good case study in your "profitable ignorance" frame, since NAM and pyruvate are cheap and unpatentable, yet a professional society is now writing position statements on them largely on academic/foundation-funded trial money.
