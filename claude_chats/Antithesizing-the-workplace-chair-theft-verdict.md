———
This is a fun one. My take:

**The chair thief is clearly TA.** He took someone else's personal property, refused to return it when asked, and then doubled down. That's straightforward theft. The fact that it happened at work doesn't make it less of a theft.

**The boss is also TA.** His "first come first serve" response to an employee explaining their personal property was stolen is absurd. He either didn't listen, didn't care, or was actively protecting the thief. Firing someone for reporting a theft to police is a bad look.

**OP is... complicated.** Legally and morally, they were in the right to recover their property. But there's a whiff of disproportionate satisfaction here that makes me wonder if this was really about the chair or about winning a confrontation. Pressing charges when you've already got your chair back, knowing it will end someone's 15-year career, is a choice. A defensible choice, but still a choice that goes beyond just protecting your property.

The "I don't give a shit" at the end is telling. OP clearly feels righteous, and honestly? They're mostly justified. But the glee about destroying someone's career suggests this became personal fast.

**Verdict:** NTA, but barely. The thief brought this on himself, and OP wasn't obligated to absorb the loss or let it slide. But I'd probably respect OP more if they seemed even slightly conflicted about the outcome rather than triumphant.
