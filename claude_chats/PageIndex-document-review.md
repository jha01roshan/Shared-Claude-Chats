## Federal Reserve's Total Deferred Asset in 2023

**$133.318 billion** (or $133,318 million)

This represents a significant increase from 2022, when the deferred asset was $16.585 billion ($16,585 million).

### What is a Deferred Asset?

A deferred asset represents the amount of net excess earnings Reserve Banks will need to realize in the future before remittances to the Treasury can resume. This occurs when the Reserve Banks' earnings are not sufficient to cover the costs of operations, payment of dividends, and maintaining the required surplus.

During 2022 and continuing through 2023, most Reserve Banks suspended their weekly remittances to the Treasury and began accumulating this deferred asset. In 2023, the Reserve Banks only transferred $670 million to the Treasury, compared to much larger amounts in previous years.

### Why Did This Happen?

In 2023, the Federal Reserve Banks experienced a net loss of $114.3 billion before providing remittances to the Treasury, compared with net income of $58.8 billion in 2022. This was primarily due to significantly higher interest expenses paid on depository institution deposits ($176.8 billion) and on securities sold under repurchase agreements ($104.3 billion), as interest rates rose substantially.
