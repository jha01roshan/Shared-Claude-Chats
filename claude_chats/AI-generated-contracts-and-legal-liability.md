## Human

If AI helps me write a contract and it goes wrong, am I liable?

---

## Assistant

Generally, yes — you're the one who signs and uses the contract, so you typically bear the liability, not the AI tool.

A few things that matter:

- **Contracts are legal instruments.** Courts look at what was signed and agreed to, not who or what drafted it. If you send it out and it's used, you own the outcome.
- **AI tools aren't liable parties.** Terms of service for AI products (Claude included) generally disclaim liability for the accuracy of generated content and put responsibility on the user to review outputs.
- **Professional indemnity insurance** may or may not cover errors introduced by AI-assisted drafting — worth checking your policy wording if you have one.
- **The real risk is what AI commonly gets wrong**: jurisdiction-specific requirements, unenforceable clauses, missing statutory protections (e.g. for employees under Australian law), or outdated references.

Given you run a business with staff, the safest pattern is: use AI to draft or speed up a first pass, then have a solicitor review anything that creates binding obligations (employment contracts, supplier agreements, client terms) before it's signed or sent.

I'm not a lawyer, so for anything with real exposure, worth a quick check with one — even a fixed-fee review can catch issues before they become expensive.
