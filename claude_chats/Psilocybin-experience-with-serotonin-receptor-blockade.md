## Assistant

This is a well-studied pharmacology question, so let me answer directly.

Ketanserin is the classic tool used in research to demonstrate that psilocybin's psychedelic effects are mediated primarily through the serotonin 5-HT2A receptor. It's a competitive antagonist with high affinity for 5-HT2A (and 5-HT2C), plus some antihistamine and alpha-1 adrenergic activity at higher doses.

**The short version:** if you take a meaningful dose of ketanserin before or alongside psilocybin, the subjective psychedelic experience is largely abolished or dramatically attenuated. This has been shown repeatedly in controlled human studies (Vollenweider's group in the late 1990s, and more recently Preller, Kraehenmann, and others).

**What gets blocked**

With adequate 5-HT2A blockade, most of the hallmark subjective effects don't materialize:

The visual phenomena — geometric patterns, drifting, intensification of color, morphing of surfaces, closed-eye imagery — are strongly 5-HT2A-dependent and are among the most reliably suppressed. The alterations in meaning and salience — the sense that things are profound, charged, or significant — largely fail to appear. Ego dissolution and the "oceanic boundlessness" dimension (the mystical-type experience) are blunted. Changes in the sense of time, audio-visual synesthesia, and the general shift in state of consciousness are all reduced.

In practical terms, someone pretreated with ketanserin who takes psilocybin often reports feeling comparatively normal — perhaps some mild residual effects, mild bodily sensations, or subtle mood shifts, but not a "trip." The subjective ceiling is pulled way down. This is the core evidence that 5-HT2A agonism is necessary for the classic psychedelic state, not merely correlated with it.

**5-HT2A vs 5-HT2C — teasing them apart**

This is where the picture gets genuinely murky, and it's worth being honest about the limits of what we know.

Ketanserin itself is *not* clean between 2A and 2C — it has appreciable affinity for both, though it's usually described as 2A-preferring (roughly an order of magnitude selectivity for 2A over 2C, depending on the assay). So a ketanserin study doesn't cleanly isolate 2A. The reason the field attributes the blocked effects mainly to 2A rather than 2C comes from converging evidence: dose-response relationships, receptor occupancy data, and studies with other, more selective compounds.

The genuinely 2A-selective picture comes partly from tools like **MDL 100907 (volinanserin)**, which is much more 2A-selective than ketanserin. Where it's been examined, high 2A selectivity is sufficient to block the core psychedelic effects, reinforcing that 2A is the necessary node.

For **5-HT2C specifically**, the honest answer is that its contribution to the human subjective experience of psilocybin is poorly characterized and probably secondary. 2C is thought to modulate things like mood, appetite, anxiety, and dopaminergic tone, and it may shape the *emotional texture* or peripheral/autonomic aspects of the experience rather than generating the core perceptual and noetic phenomena. But there isn't a clean human study where someone took psilocybin under selective 2C blockade with 2A left untouched, so strong claims here would be overreaching. Most of what's said about 2C's role is inferred from animal work and from receptor-binding profiles, not from direct human subjective reports.

A few worthwhile caveats on the whole picture:

The dose relationship matters — partial blockade produces partial attenuation, not a clean on/off. With low ketanserin doses or high psilocybin doses you can get a muted-but-present experience rather than nothing.

The "abolished" framing comes from group-averaged data in modest psilocybin doses. Individual variability is real, and I'd be cautious about assuming the effect is literally total in every person.

Ketanserin's own effects (sedation, some hypotension from alpha-1 blockade) can color the baseline, so "feeling different but not psychedelic" isn't purely the absence of a trip.

One thing worth flagging directly, in case this is more than academic: combining serotonergic antagonists with psychedelics outside a controlled research setting isn't a well-characterized safety space, and ketanserin has real cardiovascular effects of its own. If any of this is heading toward practical use rather than understanding the pharmacology, that's a different and much dicier conversation.

Is your interest here the receptor-level mechanism, the phenomenology, or something about the 2A/2C dissociation specifically? I can go deeper on whichever — the Preller work on how 2A blockade specifically affects self-experience and "meaning attribution" is particularly interesting if that's the thread you're pulling.

Good — that's the more interesting question, and it's underdiscussed precisely because the studies were designed to show the *blockade*, so the residual effects tend to get reported as afterthoughts or buried in the supplementary data.

Here's what the human literature (and the pharmacology) suggests actually persists.

**Autonomic and somatic effects**

These are the most robust survivors. Psilocybin still produces measurable physiological arousal that ketanserin doesn't fully cancel — elevated blood pressure and heart rate, pupil dilation, and general sympathetic activation. Subjectively this can register as a sense of bodily activation, restlessness, warmth, or a vague "something is happening in my body" feeling even when the mind stays relatively clear. Some of the classic somatic side effects — mild nausea, a sense of physical unease, yawning, changes in thermoregulation — are not primarily 2A-mediated and can still show up.

**Mood and affective shifts**

This is the most interesting residual category. There's evidence that some of psilocybin's effect on mood is not fully abolished by 2A blockade, which points toward the other receptors psilocin actually binds. Psilocin is a partial agonist at **5-HT1A**, and 5-HT1A activation has genuine anxiolytic and mood-modulating effects that ketanserin does nothing to block. So a person can experience mild mood elevation, a sense of calm or emotional softening, or conversely some anxiety, without the perceptual trip. The affective tone can be present while the "content" that usually drives it is absent — which people sometimes describe as a free-floating or unexplained mood shift.

**5-HT1A-attributable phenomena more broadly**

Because 1A sits entirely outside ketanserin's blockade, whatever psilocin does through 1A remains on the table: some of the relaxation, drowsiness, and the mild dreamy or introspective quality. This is worth emphasizing because it's the mechanistic reason the experience isn't a total blank — psilocybin is a dirty drug, and ketanserin only cleans up one receptor family.

**Subtle cognitive and attentional effects**

The reports here are softer and less consistent, but some participants describe mild changes that don't rise to the level of a psychedelic state — a slight looseness of association, mild distractibility, a subjectively altered relationship to their own thoughts, or a faint sense of mental "differentness." Whether this is genuine residual pharmacology or expectancy/set effects is hard to disentangle in the data, and I'd hold it loosely.

**The texture of the whole thing**

Putting it together: the characteristic subjective report of psilocybin-under-ketanserin is something like *feeling that a drug is present in the body without the mind being taken anywhere*. The perceptual and noetic machinery is offline, but the autonomic signature and the non-2A affective effects leak through. It's closer to "I can tell I took something" than to "nothing happened" — but the *something* is diffuse and bodily rather than experiential in the psychedelic sense.

Two honesty caveats worth keeping:

The residual effects are also the ones most vulnerable to confounding. Ketanserin's own pharmacology (sedation, hypotension from alpha-1 blockade, antihistamine drowsiness) contributes to the somatic/drowsy picture, so not all of what "survives" is cleanly attributable to psilocybin acting through non-2A receptors. Disentangling "psilocybin's residual 1A effect" from "ketanserin making you sleepy" is genuinely hard.

And the dose dependence cuts both ways — with incomplete 2A occupancy (lower ketanserin, higher psilocybin), some faint perceptual effects can break through, so "no visuals at all" is the clean high-occupancy case rather than a universal.

That would move this from mechanism-based reasoning to actual reported numbers, if that's the level of rigor you're after.

I have what I need. The Carter attention work is the single most useful finding — it's a rare case of a measurable psilocybin effect that ketanserin demonstrably *failed* to block, which gives concrete grounding to the "what survives" question.

Here's what the actual data show, moving from mechanism-based reasoning to what was measured.

**The subjective-scale ceiling: near-total suppression**

The foundational Vollenweider 1998 study pretreated healthy volunteers with ketanserin (20–40 mg) before psilocybin (~0.25 mg/kg) and measured subjective effects on the APZ/OAV scale, which breaks the experience into three dimensions: oceanic boundlessness (OSE — derealization/depersonalization with euphoria), visionary restructuralization (VUS — illusions, hallucinations, changed meaning), and anxious ego-dissolution (AIA — thought disorder, paranoia). Ketanserin dose-dependently, and at higher doses almost completely, blocked psilocybin's effects across these dimensions — one secondary summary puts the prevention of the core symptoms at roughly 98–100% at optimal doses. So on the *self-report* instruments, there isn't much of a quantified residual to report: the whole point of the study was that the subjective trip essentially flatlines. That's the honest headline. Where it gets interesting is the effects measured by means *other* than the ASC questionnaire.

**The clearest surviving effect: attentional impairment (Carter et al., 2005)**

This is the best-documented residual, and it's a genuinely measured one rather than an inference. Carter and colleagues gave eight volunteers placebo, psilocybin (215 µg/kg), ketanserin (50 mg), and psilocybin + ketanserin, then tested them on a multiple-object tracking task. Psilocybin significantly reduced attentional tracking ability but had no significant effect on spatial working memory. Critically, pretreatment with ketanserin did not attenuate the attentional impairment — pointing to the 5-HT1A receptor rather than 5-HT2A as the primary driver of that deficit.

So here's a concrete answer to your question: under ketanserin, a person taking psilocybin still shows measurably degraded attention — specifically, what the authors interpret as a reduced ability to suppress or ignore distracting stimuli rather than a reduction in raw attentional capacity. Subjectively this maps onto something people can report even when the visual trip is gone: participants said they could still understand the task instructions, but generally found the attention task substantially more challenging under the drug. That "everything is harder to hold onto / more distractible" quality is a residual you'd actually notice.

**Emotional/cognitive processing also partly survives**

A 2024 systematic review generalizes the Carter-type finding: studies using ketanserin pretreatment did not show significant differences in emotional processing or attentional processing being abolished, implicating the 5-HT1A receptor in those cognitive changes. The review's framing is the mechanistic summary of your whole question: 5-HT2A receptors predominantly mediate the perceptual and psychedelic effects, while 5-HT1A receptors appear to modulate cognitive and emotional processing. So the affective/cognitive layer has a 1A-mediated component that ketanserin leaves substantially intact, even as it strips out the perceptual layer.

**The autonomic/vascular residual — and one interesting wrinkle**

The recent Larsen et al. (2025) cerebral blood flow study gives a clean physiological contrast. Psilocin levels and subjective drug intensity were strongly associated with reduced cerebral blood flow (~11.5% at peak), while ketanserin given alone produced no significant CBF change. That tells you the central vascular signature of psilocybin is largely a 2A phenomenon. But note the direction of the peripheral vascular story is the opposite of what you might guess: 5-HT2A receptors on vascular smooth muscle drive serotonin-induced vasoconstriction, and ketanserin abolishes those contractions — acting as a vasodilator in the presence of serotonin. So ketanserin doesn't add to psilocybin's cardiovascular load through the 2A pathway; if anything it blunts that specific component. The sympathetic-arousal side (heart rate, some blood-pressure effects, the "activated body" feeling) runs partly through non-2A routes and is the part more likely to leak through — but I'd flag that the clean CBF data are about the brain, and I'm reasoning about the peripheral autonomic residual rather than citing a direct measurement of it.

**Pulling the numbers together into an answer**

If you want the honest quantified picture rather than the mechanism story:

On the standard subjective ASC/APZ scales, the residual is close to nil — high-dose ketanserin knocks the three core dimensions down by something like 90–100%. This is why "the trip is abolished" is basically accurate as a first-order statement.

The measurable exceptions come from tasks the questionnaire doesn't capture: attentional tracking is impaired *to a statistically significant degree that ketanserin does not reduce at all* (Carter), and emotional/cognitive processing shifts partly survive. These are the 5-HT1A-attributable residuals, and they're the concrete grounding for the "affective and cognitive texture without the perceptual trip" picture I sketched before.

One caveat on the strength of the attention finding: n=8, a single task, one timepoint (120 min). It's the best direct evidence that *something* measurable survives ketanserin, but it's a small study and I wouldn't want to overstate its precision. The direction of the effect (survives; implicates 1A) is echoed by the broader review literature, which is what gives me confidence in the pattern even if the exact magnitude is soft.
