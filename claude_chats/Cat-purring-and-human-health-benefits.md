## Human

Is there scientific evidence that a purring cat can have a health benefit for humans

---

## Assistant

Yes, there's some evidence, though it's more preliminary than definitive.

**The main theory:** Cat purrs typically fall in the 25–150 Hz range. Frequencies in that range have been linked in other research to improved bone density and tissue healing, which is why some researchers speculate purring might aid bone/tissue repair in cats and possibly humans nearby. This is often called "vibrational therapy," but direct human studies on cat purring specifically are sparse.

**Better-supported benefits (mostly from cat ownership studies, not purring alone):**
- Lower stress and cortisol levels from petting/bonding with a cat
- Studies have associated cat ownership with reduced cardiovascular risk, including one often-cited study linking cat ownership to lower rates of heart attack and stroke death
- Lower blood pressure and heart rate during interaction with a calm, purring cat
- Reduced anxiety and improved mood, likely tied to touch, companionship, and routine rather than the purr sound itself

**The honest caveat:** Much of this research is observational (correlation, not proven causation), sample sizes are often small, and it's hard to separate the effect of purring specifically from the broader benefits of petting, companionship, and stress relief. The "purr = healing frequency" claim is popular but not rigorously proven in humans.

So: cats seem genuinely good for stress and heart health, but it's more likely the overall companionship and interaction driving that than the purr sound in isolation.
