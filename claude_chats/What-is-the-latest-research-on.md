Here's a summary of the latest migraine drug research from PubMed (2024-2025):

## CGRP-Targeting Therapies

**Fremanezumab and sleep quality**: A 6-month follow-up study found fremanezumab significantly improved sleep quality in chronic migraine patients, with benefits appearing by month 3. Patients with higher anxiety, depression, and disability scores had worse sleep initially but still benefited from treatment. ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clineuro.2025.109261))

**Ubrogepant vs. triptans**: The UNIVERSE II real-world study compared patients switching from a triptan to either another triptan or ubrogepant. Those switching to ubrogepant reported significantly better satisfaction with pain treatment (69% vs 42%), relief of their most bothersome symptom, and meaningful relief at 2 hours. They were also more likely to prefer their new treatment over their prior triptan. ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/17581869.2025.2585269))

## Novel Drug Design

**Dual-target CGRP antagonist/5-HT1F agonist**: Researchers designed compound PCC0105005, which simultaneously blocks CGRP receptors and activates 5-HT1F receptors. This approach aims to enhance efficacy while limiting CNS side effects like somnolence by restricting activity to the periphery. The compound showed promising results in preclinical models. ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejmech.2025.118361))

## Gut-Brain Axis

A study on pediatric migraine found distinct gut microbiota signatures in children with migraine, including reduced *Faecalibacterium prausnitzii*. Almost half had gastrointestinal disorders. Probiotic supplementation (*Bifidobacterium longum*) showed promise in reducing headache days, intensity, and frequency in a pilot study—suggesting the gut-brain axis may be a therapeutic target. ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/19490976.2025.2606487))

## Interventional Treatments

**Nerve blocks**: Weekly vs. biweekly greater occipital and supraorbital nerve blocks both produced substantial improvements in pain, headache frequency, and disability at 3 months. The biweekly schedule showed similar efficacy with better patient adherence, potentially reducing treatment burden. ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jocn.2025.111726))

## Drug Interactions

**GLP-1 agonists and Botox**: Computational modeling suggests GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, tirzepatide, etc.) may reduce botulinum toxin durability in chronic migraine patients—potentially shortening treatment intervals from ~14 weeks to 11.8-12.6 weeks. This is theoretical and needs clinical validation, but worth noting given the widespread use of GLP-1 drugs. ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.toxicon.2025.108638))

## Placebo Response

A meta-analysis of 84 acute and 42 prophylactic migraine trials (1988-2024) found placebo response rates of 13% for "pain free" and 35% for "pain relief" in acute trials, with 28% achieving ≥50% reduction in monthly migraine days in prevention trials. Notably, placebo responses were nearly twice as high in children. ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ncl.2025.08.011))
