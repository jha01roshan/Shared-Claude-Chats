## Human

I'm considering vibe coding a prototype that makes use of the company's product in some way (e.g. if I apply to Vercel, I use V0; if I apply to ElevenLabs, I incorporate their product via API). ## Assistant

Before suggesting alternatives, I need to understand your situation better:

1. **What specific roles are you targeting?** (engineering, PM, research, etc.) The most effective way to stand out varies significantly by role type.

2. **What's your timeline and capacity?** How many companies are you applying to, and how much time can you realistically dedicate per application?

3. **What does "standing out" mean for your goals?** Are you trying to get past resume screens, impress in interviews, or demonstrate deep product understanding?

Regarding your prototype idea - it has merit but consider the trade-offs:
- **Time investment vs. impact**: Building meaningful prototypes for multiple companies could take weeks. Will each prototype be sophisticated enough to actually impress teams who see their own product used daily?
- **Differentiation risk**: Many candidates likely have the same idea. Unless your prototype demonstrates genuinely novel use cases or exceptional execution, it might blend in.

The real question is: What unique value can you demonstrate that's both authentic to your strengths and relevant to each company's specific needs? Building prototypes might be the answer, but only if you can execute at a level that genuinely surprises people familiar with their own tools.

## Assistant

For PM roles specifically, building prototypes is actually misaligned with what hiring managers look for. PMs aren't evaluated on coding ability - they're evaluated on strategic thinking, user insight, and execution ability. Your prototype strategy might even backfire by suggesting you don't understand the PM role.

Here's what actually moves the needle for PM applications at AI companies:

**1. Write a one-page strategic analysis of their product**
- Pick one specific feature or market opportunity they're missing
- Show your framework for prioritization (market size, technical feasibility, competitive advantage)
- Include rough metrics/calculations
- Time: 2-4 hours per company

**2. Create a competitive intelligence brief**
- Map their position against 3-4 competitors on specific dimensions
- Identify their strategic vulnerabilities
- Propose defensive moves
- Time: 3-5 hours per company

**3. Document user pain points with evidence**
- Scrape Reddit, Discord, support forums for actual user complaints
- Categorize and quantify the issues
- Propose solutions with clear success metrics
- Time: 2-3 hours per company

**4. Ship something small but public**
- Write a detailed blog post analyzing an AI product space
- Create a simple dashboard tracking AI model performance/pricing
- Build a directory of use cases for specific AI tools
- Time: One-time investment that applies to multiple applications

The key difference: These demonstrate PM thinking (market analysis, user empathy, prioritization) rather than engineering skills. They're also easier to execute well since you're playing to PM strengths.

Which of these aligns best with your existing strengths? And what's your current experience level - are you transitioning into PM or already in the role?

Here are additional approaches that attack the problem from different angles:

**5. Run a micro-experiment and share results**
- Test a specific hypothesis about AI product adoption (e.g., "Do users prefer chatbots with personalities?")
- Use existing tools (Typeform, social media polls, guerrilla user testing)
- Package findings in a 1-page insight report
- Time: 4-6 hours including write-up

**6. Create a "PM tear-down" video/Loom**
- Record yourself analyzing their onboarding flow or core feature
- Point out specific friction points with screenshots
- Suggest A/B tests with clear hypotheses
- Time: 1-2 hours per company

**7. Map their API/platform ecosystem opportunities**
- Identify the top 10 integrations they should build
- Estimate TAM for each integration
- Show which would drive most retention/expansion
- Time: 2-3 hours per company

**8. Start a niche community or newsletter**
- "Weekly AI Product Launches" with your quick PM take on each
- Slack/Discord for "AI PMs" discussing specific challenges
- LinkedIn newsletter analyzing AI product strategies
- Time: 2-3 hours/week, compounds over time

**9. Contribute to their community**
- Answer questions in their Discord/forum with PM-quality responses
- Create templates/frameworks other users find valuable
- Build relationships with their team naturally
- Time: Ongoing but flexible

**10. Design a partner/channel strategy**
- Identify 3-5 specific companies they should partner with
- Define the win-win value prop
- Mock up a simple partnership proposal
- Time: 2-3 hours per company

Which resonates with your style? The best approach is one you'll actually execute well, not just the one that sounds most impressive.
