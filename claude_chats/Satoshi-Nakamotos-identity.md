Satoshi Nakamoto is the pseudonymous person or group who created Bitcoin. Key facts:

**What we know for certain:**
- Published the Bitcoin whitepaper in October 2008 titled *"Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System"*
- Launched the Bitcoin network in January 2009, mining the genesis block
- Corresponded with early developers via email and forums until around 2010–2011, then disappeared
- Controls an estimated ~1 million BTC (never moved), worth tens of billions of dollars today

**Identity:** Unknown. Satoshi communicated only in writing, used British spellings, and had activity patterns suggesting either European or East Asian time zones. Numerous people have been proposed as candidates — Hal Finney, Nick Szabo, Adam Back, Len Sassaman, Craig Wright (who has falsely claimed to be Satoshi) — but none have been conclusively proven.

The mystery remains one of the most fascinating open questions in technology history.

Here's a rundown of the top 10 most-discussed Satoshi candidates, roughly ordered by credibility of the evidence:

---

**1. Nick Szabo**
Many consider Szabo the most likely candidate — his ideas around digital cash ("Bit Gold") and smart contracts closely align with Bitcoin's philosophy. Linguistic analysis of Satoshi's writing has repeatedly pointed to Szabo as the closest stylistic match. He denies it.

**2. Adam Back**
Inventor of Hashcash, the proof-of-work system directly cited in the Bitcoin whitepaper. A 2026 New York Times investigation suggested Bitcoin's creator could be Back, though he has explicitly denied it. In a 2025 interview he joked that rumors about him being Satoshi were "not crazy," given he was one of the few developers working on digital money systems at the time.

**3. Hal Finney**
The first person to receive a Bitcoin transaction. His coding style and communication tone resemble Satoshi's, and some developers say their emails share similarities. He was capable of building Bitcoin alone given his expertise. However, his early Bitcoin emails show him learning *from* Satoshi, not acting as the creator, and he was physically declining from ALS during Bitcoin's early development.

**4. Len Sassaman**
A cypherpunk cryptographer who died in 2011 — the same year Satoshi disappeared. Sassaman was deeply connected to the cypherpunk movement, used British English, and had close ties to Hal Finney. His death makes definitive confirmation impossible.

**5. Paul Le Roux**
A wildcard who emerged from the Craig Wright trial. The first hint came when Wright's lawyers accidentally left an unredacted footnote in a court filing linking to Le Roux's Wikipedia page. Le Roux was a skilled cryptographic programmer, a contributor on the Cypherpunk mailing list, and used a mixture of American and British English similar to Satoshi's. The fact that Satoshi's ~1 million BTC has never been touched is circumstantially consistent with Le Roux being imprisoned since 2012. However, writing style analyses have not matched him to Satoshi.

**6. Peter Todd**
The subject of HBO's 2024 documentary "Money Electric: The Bitcoin Mystery," Todd is a Canadian cryptographer and early Bitcoin core developer. Todd himself called the claims "ridiculous" and went on X after the documentary aired to flatly deny it. The community broadly found the documentary's evidence unconvincing.

**7. Dorian Nakamoto**
His name is literally "Dorian Prentice Satoshi Nakamoto," which triggered a 2014 Newsweek cover story. He is a physicist and systems engineer but has consistently denied any involvement, and the evidence against him is thin beyond the name coincidence.

**8. Craig Wright**
Wright claimed to be Satoshi as far back as 2015, but researchers discovered his blog entries were likely post-dated and his cryptographic proofs were insufficient. A British court ruled decisively against him in 2024, finding overwhelming evidence that he was not Satoshi. He retracted his claims that same year. Now considered a fraud by the community.

**9. David Kleiman**
A computer security expert and close associate of Craig Wright. Some point to similarities between his and Satoshi's coding style. However, the theory was weakened during Wright's trial, which found much of the evidence connecting either Wright or Kleiman to Bitcoin's creation was doctored.

**10. Elon Musk**
A fringe candidate. A former SpaceX intern argued in 2017 that Musk had both a preference for and excellent grasp of C++, like Satoshi. Musk denied it. The theory is not taken seriously by most researchers.

---

The consensus shortlist among serious researchers is **Szabo, Back, Finney, and Sassaman** — all cypherpunk-adjacent cryptographers with the right skills and timing. The identity remains unproven.
