Here's a thorough breakdown of the Form 10 and Form 21 situation in Queensland, and what it means for Adam's contravene banning notice criminal charge.

## The Core Distinction — Form 10 vs Form 21

These are both witness summons forms under the *Justices Act 1886* (Qld), but they serve entirely different parties.

**Form 10 — Summons to a Witness (prosecution/prosecution-adjacent)**

The Form 10 is issued under the *Justices Act 1886*, s.78 and is directed at a witness required to appear before the Magistrates Court in connection with a complaint. The form tells the witness: "You are required to give evidence in court for the Prosecution." It also contains logistical provisions stating that travel and accommodation arrangements for witnesses are made by the Queensland Police Service.

This is the key issue with the registrar's direction. The Form 10 is structurally a prosecution instrument. It literally has the words "for the Prosecution" printed on it, and it routes witness logistics through Queensland Police. If Adam is the defendant in a contravene banning notice charge, being told to use Form 10 to summon his own defence witnesses is procedurally odd at best — it's the wrong form for the wrong party.

**Form 21 — The Defence/Accused's Witness Summons**

Form 21 under the *Justices Act 1886* is the equivalent form for a defendant/accused to compel the attendance of their own witnesses. It serves the same compulsory attendance function as Form 10 but is issued on behalf of the defendant rather than the complainant/prosecution. The Act's framework makes a clear distinction between "prosecutor's and complainant's witnesses" and the accused's right to call their own witnesses.

In Queensland Magistrates Court criminal proceedings, the document used to compel a witness to attend is called a "summons" (as distinct from a "subpoena," which is the term used in the District and Supreme Courts). Both prosecution and defence can use this mechanism — if summonsed and the witness doesn't attend, the court may find them in contempt and issue a warrant for their arrest.

## Why This Matters Practically for Adam

The fact the registrar directed Adam to Form 10 raises a few possibilities:

1. **Clerical/administrative shorthand.** Many registry staff treat Form 10 as the generic "witness summons" and aren't thinking about the prosecution/defence distinction in the form's wording. In practice, the operational difference between Forms 10 and 21 at the registry counter can be minimal — both get stamped and issued. But the ideological problem remains: the form says "for the Prosecution."

2. **Adam is the defendant, not the complainant.** In a contravene banning notice charge, police are the complainant/prosecutor. Adam is the accused. He should be using the defendant's witness summons form, not the prosecution one. If this ever became an issue in the hearing — if a witness or the magistrate questioned the form's authority — having used the wrong form could create a problem.

3. **The correct position:** Adam should be using Form 21 for his defence witnesses, or at minimum should note on the record that he is using the summons mechanism as a defendant compelling his own witnesses, not as a prosecution witness summons.

## What Adam Should Do

He should go back to the registry and specifically ask for the **defendant's witness summons form** — Form 21. He can reference that he is the defendant in the matter, not the complainant, and that Form 10 is designated for prosecution witnesses. If the registry insists Form 10 is what they use for everyone, Adam should still fill it in but make clear in the body of the form or in accompanying correspondence that it is being issued on behalf of the defendant for defence witnesses.

He should also note the following for the hearing itself:

Under Queensland law, the defendant in a criminal matter is not required to give or call evidence, because the onus is on the prosecution to prove the case beyond reasonable doubt. However, a defendant may choose to call witnesses to provide a contradictory account or raise a defence not otherwise open on the prosecution case.

If Adam calls witnesses this way, there is a cost consideration: a defendant might be required to pay the filing fee for any summons filed.

## Enforcement Teeth

If a witness who is summonsed neglects or refuses to appear at the time and place appointed, and no just excuse is offered, the justices may impose a fine in their absence, and may also issue a warrant to bring that person before the court at a later time and place to testify.

So the summons has real compulsory force — this isn't a polite request. If Adam needs a witness who is reluctant to attend (say, a police officer, a body corporate rep, whoever administered the banning notice, or a witness to the alleged contravention), the Form 21 summons is the mechanism to compel them.

Witnesses Adam might want to summon could include the person who issued or served the banning notice, any police officer who gave Adam notice of its existence, or anyone present at the alleged contravention. All of those should be called using the defendant's summons (Form 21), not the prosecution's Form 10.
