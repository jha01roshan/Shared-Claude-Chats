## Assistant

Looking at the evidence from both interviews, Hoffman's characterization of Cade Metz appears strongly justified.

**Key supporting evidence:**

1. **Focus on social connections over substance**: Throughout the Vassar interview, Metz persistently asks about social relationships ("how did you meet Peter Thiel," "how did he invest in your company") while showing no interest in the actual ideas or content. When Vassar tries to explain what MetaMed was actually doing in response to Robin Hanson's work on medicine, Metz immediately redirects to asking about journalistic standards instead of engaging with the substance.

2. **Facts determined by credentialed authorities**: When discussing quantum mechanics, Metz explicitly states: "In that case, yeah. So people who understand the field, I think that they're, talking to them about that is valid." He defines facts as what PhD physicists say, matching Hoffman's observation that Metz sees experts as "whoever is vouched for as an expert."

3. **"Fairness" without truth-seeking**: Metz repeatedly defines good journalism as "look at this from all sides" and "give everyone their due." When Vassar asks him directly "What would getting it right mean?" Metz cannot answer beyond saying "we are journalists with a lot of experience."

4. **Active avoidance of epistemological questions**: The most striking pattern is Metz's consistent deflection whenever Vassar tries to discuss how we determine truth. When Vassar asks about "differences between the norms of reason," Metz refuses to engage, saying "I don't think that the comparison is what I'm trying to do here." When pressed on what facts are, he gives only procedural answers about talking to people.

5. **The "perfect aggression" observation**: As the Hazard annotations note, Metz's responses often seem like placating non-sequiturs that don't engage with what's being asked. His repeated "I hear you" and "I'm with you" responses while obviously not understanding or engaging with Vassar's points could indeed be seen as "perfect aggression against anyone who cares about anything."

The Zack Davis interview provides additional confirmation - when Davis points out apparent misrepresentation in Metz's article, Metz's defense is merely that it was "fact-checked" and that "what I wrote was true" while admitting "there are different ways of interpreting it."

Vassar's summary seems apt: Metz operates with "fundamentally postmodern epistemology" where "facts are socially constructed, based on patterns of authority, the details of which are to be concealed." The interviews show Metz treating journalism as collecting and balancing social perspectives rather than attempting to understand or convey truth about reality.
