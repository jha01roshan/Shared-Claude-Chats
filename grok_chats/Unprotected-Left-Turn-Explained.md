## Assistant

An **unprotected left turn** is a common driving maneuver at an intersection where you turn left without a dedicated traffic signal (like a green arrow) giving you the right-of-way.

### Key Characteristics
- It occurs when the traffic light shows a solid **green circle** (or sometimes a flashing yellow arrow) for your direction, but **no green left-turn arrow**.
- In contrast, a **protected left turn** uses a green arrow, which stops oncoming traffic and gives you exclusive right-of-way to turn safely.
- Unprotected turns require you to **yield** to oncoming vehicles (going straight or turning right) and pedestrians in the crosswalk.

### Rules and Right-of-Way
In most jurisdictions (rules are similar across the US and many other places):
- Oncoming traffic has the **right-of-way**.
- You must wait for a safe gap in traffic — large enough to complete the turn without forcing others to slow down or stop suddenly.
- You also yield to pedestrians crossing the road you're turning into.
- Once in the intersection (if safe), you can complete the turn even if the light turns red afterward (as long as you entered on green).
- Left-turning drivers are often presumed at fault in collisions with oncoming vehicles, since they must ensure the path is clear.

### How to Make an Unprotected Left Turn Safely
1. **Approach the intersection** — Signal early, move into the left-turn lane if available, and stop behind the stop line if needed.
2. **Check for pedestrians and bikes** first.
3. **Look for a gap** — Judge the speed and distance of oncoming traffic (both lanes if multi-lane).
4. **Enter the intersection cautiously** — If there's room, pull forward slightly (wheels straight) to wait for your gap — this is often called "taking the intersection."
5. **Turn when safe** — Accelerate smoothly and complete the turn into the correct lane.
6. **Stay alert** — Watch for aggressive drivers or those running lights.

Unprotected left turns are one of the more dangerous maneuvers because they involve crossing oncoming traffic lanes, contributing to many intersection accidents. Practice helps build confidence — always prioritize safety over rushing the turn.
