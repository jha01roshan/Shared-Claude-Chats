## Assistant

Michael Moritz is a Welsh-born billionaire venture capitalist, philanthropist, author, and former journalist. He is best known for his long tenure at Sequoia Capital, where he played a pivotal role in transforming it into one of Silicon Valley's most influential venture capital firms. As of 2025, he serves as chairman of Sequoia Heritage, an independent wealth-management fund he helped establish, after stepping down from Sequoia Capital in 2023. He was knighted in 2013 for his contributions to business and philanthropy, becoming Sir Michael Moritz.

### Educational Background
Moritz was born on September 12, 1954, in Cardiff, Wales, to a Jewish family that had fled Nazi Germany. He attended Howardian High School in Cardiff. He then pursued higher education at Christ Church, Oxford, where he earned a bachelor's degree in history. ### Professional Background
Moritz began his career as a journalist in the early 1980s, working for Time magazine as its San Francisco Bureau Chief. During this period, he covered the tech industry extensively, including a deep dive into Apple Inc. that resulted in his 1984 book, *The Little Kingdom: The Private Story of Apple Computer* (later revised and expanded in 2009 as *Return to the Little Kingdom*). He also co-authored *Going for Broke: Lee Iacocca's Battle to Save Chrysler* in 1984. His journalism career gave him early exposure to Silicon Valley's emerging tech scene, though it also led to a notable fallout with Steve Jobs over a controversial Time magazine cover story in 1982.<grok:render card_id="00577d" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation">

After leaving Time, Moritz co-founded Technologic Partners, a technology newsletter and conference company. In 1986, he transitioned to venture capital by joining Sequoia Capital as a partner, where he remained for nearly four decades until his departure in 2023 to focus on Sequoia Heritage. He stepped back from day-to-day management at Sequoia in 2012 due to a rare, incurable medical condition but continued as chairman until his exit.<grok:render card_id="dfc0d4" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation">
