## Human

I have a vehicle wheel that has been chrome plated, and the plating is starting to flake around the tire bead area.  It is causing leaks.  What are solutions to fixing this and to keep it from spreading?

---

## Assistant

Dealing with flaking chrome plating on a vehicle wheel, especially around the tire bead area, can be tricky since it’s causing leaks. Here’s a breakdown of solutions to fix the issue and prevent it from worsening:

### Fixing the Current Problem
1. **Remove the Flaking Chrome:**
   - The loose or flaking chrome needs to be removed to create a smooth, stable surface for the tire to seal against. Use a wire brush, sandpaper (start with coarse like 80-grit, then finer like 220-grit), or a media blaster (e.g., with glass beads or soda) to strip the affected area. Focus on the bead seating area where the tire seals.
   - Be cautious not to damage the wheel’s structural integrity—avoid excessive grinding.

2. **Seal the Bead Area:**
   - After removing the loose chrome, clean the area thoroughly with a degreaser (like acetone or brake cleaner) to remove any residue.
   - Apply a bead sealer or tire sealant (e.g., Permatex Tire Bead Sealer or a similar product) around the bead area before remounting the tire. This can help compensate for minor surface irregularities and stop leaks.
   - Alternatively, a thin layer of high-quality automotive epoxy (like JB Weld) could be applied to the stripped area, sanded smooth once cured, to create a durable sealing surface. Ensure it’s rated for metal and can withstand tire pressure.

3. **Remount and Test the Tire:**
   - Have the tire professionally remounted and balanced. Ask the technician to check for leaks using soapy water or a submersion test after inflation.

4. **Consider a Temporary Fix (if Immediate):**
   - If you need a quick patch, a tire repair shop might apply a bead sealant without stripping the chrome, but this is less reliable long-term if the flaking continues.

### Preventing Further Spreading
1. **Stop Chrome Deterioration:**
   - Chrome flaking often stems from corrosion underneath, especially if moisture or salt (from roads) has penetrated. After fixing the bead area, inspect the rest of the wheel for early signs of pitting or bubbling.
   - Apply a clear automotive sealant or protective coating (e.g., wheel-specific spray paint or a clear coat like Eastwood Diamond Clear) over the remaining chrome to slow further corrosion. Ensure the surface is clean and dry first.

2. **Replate the Wheel:**
   - For a permanent fix, consider stripping all the chrome and having the wheel professionally rechromed. This involves chemical stripping, polishing, and reapplying the chrome layer—costly (often $100-$300 per wheel), but it restores the finish and prevents future issues. Look for a reputable plating shop with experience in automotive wheels.

3. **Switch to a Different Finish:**
   - If replating isn’t worth it, strip the chrome entirely and opt for powder coating or painting. Powder coating is durable, resists corrosion, and can be done in various colors (typically $50-$150 per wheel). It’ll eliminate the chrome flaking issue entirely and still seal well with the tire.

4. **Maintenance Tips:**
   - Wash the wheels regularly to remove salt, brake dust, and grime that accelerate corrosion.
   - Avoid curb rash or impacts near the bead area, as damage to the chrome can start new flaking points.

### Recommendation
For a quick, practical fix, strip the flaking chrome around the bead, apply a bead sealer, and remount the tire. For a long-term solution, either replate the wheel or switch to powder coating, depending on your budget and aesthetic preference. If the wheel is structurally sound, these steps should stop the leaks and curb the spreading.
