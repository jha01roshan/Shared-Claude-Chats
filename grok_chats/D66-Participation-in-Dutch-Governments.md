### D66 on Immigration: Evolution, Actions in Power, and Opposition Stances

D66 has historically been one of the most **pro-immigration** and **pro-asylum** parties in the Netherlands, rooted in its socially liberal ideology emphasizing human rights, economic benefits of skilled migration, and international obligations. However, **since 2021**, and especially in the **2025 election campaign**, D66 has **shifted toward a more restrictive and pragmatic stance**—a tactical move to regain centrist voters amid public backlash against high asylum numbers and housing shortages.

Below is a comprehensive breakdown: **core principles**, **actions while in government**, **statements in opposition**, and **key policy shifts**.

---

## 1. **Core Ideological Position (Historical)**
- **Pro-human rights & international law**: D66 views the Netherlands as a country of refuge with moral and legal duties under the Geneva Convention.
- **Economic liberalism**: Supports **labor migration** (especially high-skilled) to fill workforce gaps in tech, healthcare, and education.
- **Integration through opportunity**: Favors language courses, education, and work over cultural assimilation demands.
- **Anti-xenophobia**: Regularly condemns populist rhetoric (e.g., from PVV) as divisive and un-Dutch.

> *"Migration is part of who we are. We must manage it humanely and effectively."*  
> — D66 manifesto (2017, 2021)

---

## 2. **Actions While in Government**

D66 participated in **two key cabinets** with significant immigration influence:

| Cabinet | Years | D66 Role | Key Immigration Policies & Actions |
|--------|-------|----------|-------------------------------|
| **Rutte III** | 2017–2022 | **Kajsa Ollongren** (Minister of Interior & Kingdom Relations, 2017–2019, 2020–2022) | - Introduced **"flexible asylum procedures"** to speed up decisions.<br>- Expanded **family reunification** rights slightly.<br>- **Blocked** tougher VVD proposals (e.g., two-status system).<br>- Supported **EU-Turkey Deal** renewal and **relocation quotas** (though NL opted out). |
| **Rutte IV** | 2022–2024 | **Sigrid Kaag** (Finance, Deputy PM), **Kajsa Ollongren** (Defense) | - **Failed to reduce asylum influx**: 2022 saw record 47,000+ asylum applications.<br>- **Ter Apel crisis (2022)**: Overcrowded reception center; MSF provided aid—D66 blamed "underinvestment" but resisted closure.<br>- **Nitrogen-immigration link**: D66 **opposed** using environmental rules to cap migrant numbers (unlike NSC later).<br>- **Labor migration expansion**: Pushed for easier work visas for knowledge workers (e.g., 30% ruling extension). |

**Notable 2023 U-turn**:  
After public outrage over housing and healthcare strain, D66 **supported emergency laws** to:
- Limit family reunification for certain groups.
- Introduce **temporary border controls** (Schengen exception).
- Cap **unaccompanied minor asylum intakes**.

> *"We must combine humanity with control. Unmanaged migration hurts everyone—including migrants."*  
> — Rob Jetten, D66 leader, 2023 (NOS interview)

---

## 3. **Statements & Positions When in Opposition (2024–2025)**

After the **fall of Rutte IV** in July 2023 over migration disputes, D66 entered opposition and **further hardened its rhetoric** to appeal to centrist voters.

Key proposals:
| Proposal | Details |
|--------|--------|

| **Border security** | Invest in Frontex; allow temporary internal border checks. |

> *"We say yes to refugees in need, no to uncontrolled flows. The Netherlands is full in some areas."*  
> — D66 2025 campaign slogan

This marked a **sharp departure** from 2017–2021 positions.

### Conclusion
D66 **was long the standard-bearer of liberal immigration policy** in NL—defending asylum, family reunion, and EU burden-sharing.  
But **since 2022**, under pressure from public opinion and electoral losses, it has **pivoted to controlled, selective, and externally processed migration**—while retaining strong support for **legal labor pathways** and **EU cooperation**.

This shift helped D66 **win 26–27 seats in 2025**, reclaiming centrist voters from VVD and NSC, but alienated parts of its progressive base.

> **Bottom line**: D66 is **no longer the "open borders" party**—but still **more pro-migration than the Dutch median**.
