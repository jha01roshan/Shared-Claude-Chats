### Key Categories for Media Domain Classification

Research suggests that media domains can be effectively classified into 10 primary categories based on their dominant content focus, operational models, and audience engagement. These are derived from academic typologies emphasizing functional roles, content types, and digital adaptations, acknowledging overlaps in hybrid cases.

- **Legacy News**: Traditional outlets transitioned online, prioritizing factual reporting (e.g., nytimes.com, bbc.com).
- **Digital-Native News**: Born-digital platforms with innovative storytelling (e.g., vox.com, huffpost.com).
- **Opinion and Blogs**: Focus on commentary and personal views rather than original reporting (e.g., dailywire.com blogs).
- **Academic and Journals**: Scholarly publications and research repositories (e.g., nature.com, arxiv.org).
- **Corporate and Business**: Company-promoted content for branding or services (e.g., tesla.com, openai.com).
- **Government and Official**: State-run or official information sources (e.g., nasa.gov, un.org).
- **NGO and Non-Profit**: Advocacy or think-tank content from non-governmental entities (e.g., brookings.edu).
- **Social Media and Platforms**: User-interaction driven networks (e.g., x.com, facebook.com).
- **Reference and Encyclopedias**: Factual knowledge bases (e.g., britannica.com, dictionary.com).
- **Aggregators and Portals**: Curated content from multiple sources (e.g., yahoo news, google news).

#### Rationale and Overlaps
These categories build on functional models like publishers vs. aggregators, while addressing digital shifts. Evidence leans toward flexibility, as domains may blend elements (e.g., a corporate site with blog-like opinions), but primary content guides classification.

#### Applications in Analysis
In media studies, such categories help assess bias, trust, and influence. For instance, legacy news often scores higher on professionalism, while social media may amplify disinformation.
