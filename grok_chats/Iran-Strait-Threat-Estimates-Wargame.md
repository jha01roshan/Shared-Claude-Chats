### Wargame Implications for Opening the Strait
The 48-hour presidential mandate (H-Hour 27 Mar → deadline 29 Mar) is extremely tight and high-risk. My estimates don't change the **core operational plan** in the briefing, but they do highlight why Phase 2 (threat elimination) and Phase 3 (BDA + MCM confirmation) are the make-or-break windows:

- **MCM clearance** remains the pacing item. Pre-sweep corridors 6–12 h before each tanker group is mandatory; 24/7 MQ-9 FMV + aggressive interdiction of any small craft approaching the lane is non-negotiable.  
- **FIAC/USV/MANPADS** require the full layered kill chain: E-2D AEW, F-35B/C DCA, A-10C/Apaches on strip alert, AH-64E screen, and Musandam JTAC/Sniper fires.  
- **Mobile missiles** demand persistent SIGINT/EW + rapid air strikes on any TEL movement.  
- **Ground risk** at Musandam/Khasab (Iranian SF infiltration) is real — SEAL recon + Apache-escorted MV-22B insertions are smart mitigations.  
- **Endstate realism**: A "sanitised corridor" with persistent escort and surveillance is achievable, but full commercial resumption may take days/weeks beyond the 48 h political clock. Tanker losses = strategic failure.

**Bottom line**: The briefing table is a good conservative planning tool. My adjustments (higher missile residuals, slightly adjusted small-craft numbers) make the 48-hour sprint even more demanding but still feasible under absolute air superiority and aggressive overwatch. Mines + swarms are the real demons — everything else is manageable with the force laydown provided. This wargame will turn on how fast CENTCOM can turn ISR + fires into BDA confidence before the President declares "open and safe."
