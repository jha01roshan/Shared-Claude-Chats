## Human

In what instances has the notwithstanding clause Bern used and when.

---

## Assistant

The notwithstanding clause, formally known as Section 33 of the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms, allows federal or provincial legislatures to override certain Charter rights (sections 2 and 7–15) for up to five years, subject to renewal. It has been invoked or attempted numerous times since the Charter's enactment in 1982, primarily by Quebec in its early years and more frequently by various provinces since 2018. Below is a chronological list of all known instances, including attempts that were not fully enacted or were later withdrawn, based on documented historical records.

### 1982
- **Quebec**: Invoked in the Act Respecting the Constitution Act, 1982, applying a blanket override to sections 2 and 7–15 for all existing and new laws as a protest against the Charter's patriation. It was used and its validity was upheld by the Supreme Court in *Ford v. Quebec (AG)*.
- **Yukon**: Attempted in the Land Planning and Development Act to override section 15 (equality rights) regarding committee appointments, but never brought into force.

### 1986
- **Quebec**: Invoked in An Act Respecting the Pension Plan of Certain Teachers to override section 15 (equality rights) for pension provisions; renewed seven times and set to expire in January 2025.
- **Quebec**: Invoked in An Act Respecting the Government and Public Employees Retirement Plan to override section 15; renewed seven times and set to expire in January 2025.
- **Quebec**: Invoked in An Act Respecting the Teachers Pension Plan to override section 15; renewed seven times and set to expire in January 2025.
- **Quebec**: Invoked in An Act Respecting the Civil Service Superannuation Plan to override section 15; renewed seven times and set to expire in January 2025.
- **Quebec**: Invoked in An Act Respecting the Conseil supérieur de l'Éducation to override sections 2(a) (freedom of religion) and 15; renewed in 1988, 1994, and 1999 before repeal in 2001.
- **Quebec**: Invoked in An Act Respecting the Ministère de l'Éducation to override sections 2(a) and 15; renewed in 1988, 1994, and 1999 before repeal in 2001.
- **Quebec**: Invoked in The Education Act for Cree, Inuit and Naskapi Native Persons to override sections 2(a) and 15; renewed five times before expiring on July 1, 2008.
- **Quebec**: Invoked in An Act to Amend the Act to Promote the Development of Agricultural Operations to override section 15; repealed in 1987.
- **Saskatchewan**: Invoked in the SGEU Dispute Settlement Act (back-to-work legislation) to override section 2(d) (freedom of association); brought into force but later deemed unnecessary by the Supreme Court.

### 1988
- **Quebec**: Invoked in the Education Act to override sections 2(a) and 15; renewed four times before expiring on July 1, 2008.
- **Quebec**: Invoked in An Act to Amend the Charter of the French Language (Bill 178) to override sections 2(b) (freedom of expression) and 15 regarding French-only commercial signs; brought into force but not renewed after expiry in 1993.

### 1989
- **Quebec**: Invoked in An Act Respecting School Elections to override sections 2(a) and 15; renewed in 1994 before repeal in 1999.

### 1992
- **Quebec**: Invoked in An Act Respecting Private Education to override sections 2(a) and 15; expired in 1997 without renewal.

### 1998
- **Alberta**: Attempted in the Institutional Confinement and Sexual Sterilization Compensation Act to override sections 2 and 7–15 regarding compensation limits for victims of forced sterilization, but the bill was dropped.

### 2000
- **Alberta**: Invoked in the Marriage Amendment Act to override sections 2 and 7–15 by defining marriage as opposite-sex only; declared ultra vires (beyond provincial powers) by the Supreme Court in 2004 and expired in March 2005.

### 2001
- **Quebec**: Invoked in An Act Respecting the Pension Plan of Management Personnel to override section 15; renewed four times and set to expire in January 2025.

### 2005
- **Quebec**: Invoked in the Act to Amend Various Legislative Provisions of a Confessional Nature in the Education Field to override sections 2(a) and 15; expired in July 2008.

### 2018
- **Saskatchewan**: Attempted in the School Choice Protection Act to override sections 2 and 15 regarding funding for non-Catholic students in Catholic schools; not brought into force and became unnecessary after a court ruling was overturned.
- **Ontario**: Attempted in the Efficient Local Government Act (Bill 5) to override sections 2, 7, and 15 regarding Toronto city council size reductions during an election; bill dropped after a court stay.

### 2019
- **Quebec**: Invoked in An Act Respecting the Laicity of the State (Bill 21) to override sections 2 and 7–15 banning religious symbols for public employees; received royal assent on June 16, 2019, and renewed in May 2024.
- **New Brunswick**: Attempted in An Act Respecting Proof of Immunization to override sections 2 and 7–15 for mandatory vaccinations; clause removed before the bill's defeat at third reading.

### 2021
- **Ontario**: Invoked in the Protecting Elections and Defending Democracy Act (Bill 307) to override sections 2 and 7–15 limiting third-party election advertising; received royal assent on June 14, 2021, but struck down by courts in 2023.

### 2022
- **Quebec**: Invoked in An Act Respecting French, the Official and Common Language of Québec (Bill 96) to override sections 2 and 7–15 strengthening French language requirements; received royal assent on June 1, 2022.
- **Ontario**: Invoked in the Keeping Students in Class Act (Bill 28, back-to-work legislation for education workers) to override sections 2, 7, and 15; received royal assent on November 3, 2022, but repealed and nullified retroactively on November 14, 2022.

### 2023
- **Saskatchewan**: Invoked in The Education (Parents’ Bill of Rights) Amendment Act to override sections 2, 7, and 15 requiring parental consent for name/pronoun changes and gender-related education in schools; received royal assent and came into force on October 20, 2023.

### 2025
- **Alberta**: Invoked in the Back to School Act (back-to-work legislation for teachers) to override sections 2 and 7–15; received royal assent and came into force on October 28, 2025.

The federal government has never invoked the clause. Some invocations were pre-emptive (to shield laws from anticipated challenges), while others responded to court rulings. Renewals are common for ongoing overrides, particularly in Quebec's pension and education laws.<grok:render card_id="49999b" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation">
