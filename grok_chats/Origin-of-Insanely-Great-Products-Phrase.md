## Human

What are the origins of Steve Jobs' phrase 'insanely great products'?

---

## Assistant

Steve Jobs' phrase "insanely great products" reflects his passion for creating exceptional, groundbreaking technology that went beyond mere functionality to inspire awe and delight. While there’s no single definitive moment pinpointing its first use, the phrase emerged as part of Jobs’ lexicon during his time at Apple, particularly around the development of the Macintosh in the early 1980s.

Jobs was heavily influenced by his focus on excellence and his belief that products should blend art, technology, and user experience seamlessly. The term "insanely great" is often traced back to his work with the Macintosh team, where he pushed for a computer that wasn’t just good or innovative but extraordinary—something that would leave a lasting impact. In a 1984 Rolling Stone interview, Jobs described the Macintosh as "insanely great," emphasizing its revolutionary design and interface. This ethos became a hallmark of his leadership style, both at Apple and later at Pixar, where he demanded perfection and creativity in equal measure.

The phrase itself may also echo his exposure to counterculture and his knack for dramatic, memorable language—qualities he picked up from influences like his early interest in calligraphy, Zen Buddhism, and the tech visionaries he admired, such as Edwin Land of Polaroid fame. Jobs often spoke of Land’s ability to fuse science and humanities, a philosophy that likely shaped his own pursuit of "insanely great" outcomes.

In short, "insanely great products" crystallized as Jobs’ way of articulating his relentless drive for perfection and innovation, debuting prominently with the Macintosh and sticking as a defining piece of his legacy.
