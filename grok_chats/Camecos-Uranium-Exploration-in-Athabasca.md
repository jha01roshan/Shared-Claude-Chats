Cameco, as one of the world's leading uranium producers, continues to focus on the Athabasca Basin in northern Saskatchewan, a region renowned for hosting the highest-grade uranium deposits globally. The company's prospects for discovering and developing new high-grade ore bodies in this area are tied to its extensive exploration efforts, operational expertise, and strategic projects. Here's a detailed look at Cameco's current and potential prospects based on its activities in the region as of February 23, 2025.

Cameco's exploration strategy in the Athabasca Basin centers on replacing mineral reserves depleted by production and identifying new high-grade deposits to sustain its long-term growth. The company holds interests in 39 exploration projects across the basin, leveraging its large land position—spanning about 1.9 million acres in northern Saskatchewan—and decades of experience in discovering and developing world-class uranium mines. This positions Cameco well to uncover new high-grade ore bodies, particularly given the basin's unique geological history, which has historically yielded deposits with uranium grades up to 100 times higher than global averages.

One of the most promising prospects is the Dawn Lake project, located 45 kilometers northwest of Cameco's Rabbit Lake operation. In 2023, exploration drilling at Dawn Lake expanded the footprint of known uranium mineralization, with intercepts exceeding 60% U3O8 over several meters. While still in the early stages, these high-grade results and favorable geological conditions mirror those of Cameco’s flagship operations like Cigar Lake and McArthur River, suggesting significant potential. Cameco has planned further exploration spending in 2024, allocating about $7 million to advance brownfield and advanced exploration, primarily targeting the La Rocque Lake corridor within Dawn Lake. The Tamarack deposit at Dawn Lake, with indicated resources of 17.9 million pounds U3O8 at 4.42% grade and inferred resources of 1 million pounds at 1.02%, further underscores the area's promise.

Another key prospect is the Millennium deposit, located 35 kilometers north of Cameco’s Key Lake operation in the southeastern Athabasca Basin. This undeveloped deposit is considered one of Cameco’s most significant uranium projects globally. Though specific recent updates on Millennium are limited, its high-grade nature and proximity to existing infrastructure suggest it could become a major contributor to Cameco’s portfolio once market conditions and development timelines align. The company employs a stage-gate process to evaluate such projects, factoring in economic, technical, and environmental considerations, which will determine its progression to production.

Cameco’s operational mines also offer prospects for new high-grade ore discoveries through brownfield exploration. At Cigar Lake, the world’s highest-grade uranium mine with an average grade of 14.69% U3O8, Cameco is extending the mine’s life to 2036 by targeting a new orebody. This extension requires significant investment—estimated at $895 million—starting immediately, indicating confidence in the potential for sustained high-grade output. Similarly, the McArthur River mine, the world’s largest high-grade uranium mine, has seen increased production guidance in November 2024 due to strong performance, and Cameco is considering ramping up output further by the end of the decade. Exploration near these operations could uncover additional high-grade zones, leveraging existing infrastructure to reduce development costs.

Beyond specific projects, Cameco’s regional exploration efforts target both greenfield and brownfield opportunities. In 2023, the company spent $11 million on regional programs, including geophysical surveys and diamond drilling, primarily in northern Saskatchewan. These efforts aim to identify new high-grade deposits across the basin, where uranium mineralization typically occurs at the unconformity between the Athabasca sandstone and basement rocks—conditions Cameco’s geologists are adept at exploiting.

The West McArthur joint venture with CanAlaska Uranium (Cameco owns 16.6%) highlights another high-grade prospect. Drilling at the Pike zone in early 2024 intersected exceptional mineralization, including 13.8% eU3O8 over 16.8 meters, with a peak of 40.3% over 4.7 meters. Described as rare even for the Athabasca Basin, these results suggest Pike could evolve into a tier-one deposit. Winter drilling in 2025 will focus on expanding this zone and testing along strike, potentially adding to Cameco’s high-grade resource base.

Cameco’s technological innovations, such as the Jet Boring System at Cigar Lake and its expertise in managing the basin’s challenging geology (e.g., water-saturated ground and weak rock formations), enhance its ability to develop new high-grade discoveries efficiently. The company’s strong partnerships with local Indigenous communities also ensure social license and access to prospective areas, bolstering its exploration success.

In summary, Cameco’s prospects for new high-grade ore bodies in the Athabasca Basin are robust, driven by ongoing exploration at Dawn Lake, the undeveloped Millennium deposit, extensions at Cigar Lake and McArthur River, and emerging discoveries like Pike at West McArthur. With rising global uranium demand and the basin’s proven potential, Cameco is well-positioned to capitalize on these opportunities, though timelines depend on market signals, technical feasibility, and further exploration results.
