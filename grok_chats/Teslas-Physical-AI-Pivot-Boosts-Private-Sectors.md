### Which types of companies might Tesla want to invest in or partner with to gain momentum?

Tesla is famously vertically integrated and prefers in-house development, but it **partners strategically** (suppliers, joint development, selective investments) and has done so historically (e.g., Panasonic cells, LG Energy for storage batteries, various Chinese component makers). To accelerate 2026+ momentum in its pivot areas, expect focus on:

- **Robotics component/actuator specialists** — Private firms in precision gears, motors, sensors, or dexterous manipulation tech to scale Optimus cheaply and reliably (Tesla is actively mapping this “Optimus chain”).
- **Advanced battery / energy storage tech** — Startups with novel cell chemistries, fast-charging, or recycling to feed Megapack growth and reduce costs.
- **AI chip / semiconductor enablers** — Design tools, packaging, or specialized foundry services that complement Terafab (Tesla wants faster iteration on custom silicon for FSD/Optimus).
- **Physical AI software / simulation / data** — Companies in end-to-end training pipelines, synthetic data generation, or safety validation for robots and robotaxis.
- **Supply-chain / materials innovators** — Rare-earth alternatives, lightweight composites, or vertical integration plays for robots/energy products.

In short: Tesla is signaling it has breathing room in EVs to double down on AI/robotics/energy. For VCs and emerging private companies, this is **net positive** — it creates massive new addressable markets and partnership pipelines while underscoring that pure EV plays need differentiation or adjacency to Tesla’s ecosystem to thrive. The next few quarters (actual Q1 results, Cybercab/Optimus updates) will sharpen this further.
