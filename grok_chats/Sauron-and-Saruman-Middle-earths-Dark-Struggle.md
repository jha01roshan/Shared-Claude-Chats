Here is a concise yet complete plot summary of *The Lord of the Rings*, centered on Sauron, the ancient Dark Lord, and Saruman, the wizard who fell to corruption through the palantír and his own lust for power:

In the Second Age, Sauron, the original Dark Lord and servant of Morgoth, forged the One Ring to dominate all life in Middle-earth. He was defeated when Isildur cut the Ring from his hand, but Sauron’s spirit endured because the Ring survived. For thousands of years he slowly regained strength in Mirkwood (as the Necromancer) and then returned openly to his ancient fortress of Barad-dûr in Mordor.

By the end of the Third Age, Sauron is a lidless eye of fire atop Barad-dûr, pouring all his malice and will into recovering the One Ring, the source of his power. His armies of Orcs, trolls, Haradrim, and Easterlings prepare to crush the Free Peoples (Men of Gondor and Rohan, Elves, and Dwarves) and enslave Middle-earth.

Meanwhile, Saruman the White—head of the Istari (wizards) and originally the wisest and most powerful of them—has secretly studied the lore of the Rings of Power. Through the Orthanc palantír he comes into indirect contact with Sauron, who bends the seeing-stone to his will. Saruman believes he can first ally with Sauron, then supplant him and rule Middle-earth himself with the Ring. He breeds a vast army of Uruk-hai in Isengard, industrialises the once-green valley, and begins hunting for the Ring.

The story proper begins when the One Ring, long lost, resurfaces in the possession of the hobbit Bilbo Baggins and is passed to his nephew Frodo. Gandalf the Grey discovers its true nature and realises the only way to defeat Sauron permanently is to destroy the Ring in the fires of Mount Doom in Mordor itself.

Frodo, accompanied by his friends Sam, Merry, and Pippin, leaves the Shire. The Fellowship of the Ring is formed in Rivendell: Frodo (Ring-bearer), Sam, Merry, Pippin, Aragorn (heir of Isildur), Boromir of Gondor, Gandalf, Legolas the Elf, and Gimli the Dwarf.

While the Fellowship travels south, Saruman betrays the Free Peoples openly: he tries to seize the Ring by force, attacks Rohan, and imprisons Gandalf (who escapes after becoming Gandalf the White). Sauron, unaware that the Ring is being carried toward Mordor, assumes Aragorn or some other great lord has claimed it and launches premature war against Gondor to crush possible rivals.

Major events follow:

- Saruman’s defeat at the Battle of the Hornburg (Helm’s Deep) and the destruction of Isengard by the Ents.
- Sauron’s vast armies besiege Minas Tirith; the Battle of the Pelennor Fields is won only through the arrival of Aragorn, the Rohirrim, and the Army of the Dead.
- Aragorn is crowned King Elessar and leads the hosts of the West in a desperate diversionary attack on the Black Gate to draw Sauron’s eye away from Mordor.

While the war rages, Frodo and Sam, guided and tormented by Gollum, reach Mount Doom. At the final moment Frodo claims the Ring, but Gollum bites it from his finger and falls into the fire, destroying the Ring and Sauron with it. Barad-dûr collapses, Sauron’s spirit is reduced to a powerless shadow, and his armies are thrown into chaos.

With the Dark Lord gone and Saruman later slain in the Shire by his own servant Gríma Wormtongue, the Third Age ends. The Elves depart Middle-earth, the time of the Dominion of Men begins under King Elessar, and the hobbits return home to heal their own land from Saruman’s brief but destructive occupation.

Thus the ancient Dark Lord Sauron and the rogue wizard Saruman—each seeking dominion through the Ring or its power—are both utterly defeated, and Middle-earth is freed from the shadow of the Rings forever.
