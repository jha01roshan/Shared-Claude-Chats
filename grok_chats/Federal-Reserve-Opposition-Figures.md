## Human

Who were the individuals against the foundation of the federal reserve? Briefly

---

## Assistant

Several notable figures opposed the creation of the Federal Reserve System, established in 1913, primarily due to concerns over centralized banking power, economic control, and potential overreach by the federal government. Key opponents included:

- **William Jennings Bryan**: A prominent Democratic politician and populist, he distrusted centralized banking and feared the Federal Reserve would favor wealthy elites over the common people.
- **Charles A. Lindbergh Sr.**: A Republican congressman, he argued the Federal Reserve Act would concentrate financial power in the hands of a few bankers, undermining economic freedom.
- **Louis T. McFadden**: Another congressman, he later became a vocal critic, claiming the Fed served private banking interests and lacked transparency.
- **Some Progressive and Populist Leaders**: Figures like Robert M. La Follette opposed the Fed, viewing it as a tool for corporate and banking elites to control the economy.

Opposition generally stemmed from fears of monopolistic control, loss of economic sovereignty, and potential inflation or financial instability. Despite their efforts, the Federal Reserve Act passed, shaped by debates over balancing public and private interests in monetary policy.

## Assistant

There is no definitive historical evidence that opponents of the Federal Reserve System’s creation suffered unexpected deaths directly linked to their opposition. However, a persistent conspiracy theory claims that three wealthy individuals—John Jacob Astor IV, Isidor Straus, and Benjamin Guggenheim—who died in the 1912 Titanic sinking, opposed the Federal Reserve and were deliberately targeted. This theory suggests J.P. Morgan, a key figure in the Titanic’s ownership and a supporter of centralized banking, orchestrated their deaths to remove opposition before the Federal Reserve Act passed in 1913. 

Historians and researchers, including George Behe of the Titanic Historical Society and a Washington Post investigation, have found no evidence that Astor, Straus, or Guggenheim opposed the Federal Reserve. Straus reportedly even supported it. The claim that Morgan canceled his Titanic trip hours before departure to avoid a planned sinking lacks credible documentation, and his absence is attributed to other reasons, such as business or health concerns. The theory is largely dismissed as speculative, with no reputable sources confirming these men’s stance against the Federal Reserve or any foul play in their deaths.[](https://www.reuters.com/article/fact-check/corrected-fact-check-jp-morgan-did-not-sink-the-titanic-to-push-forward-plans-idUSL1N2LF18G/)

Beyond this, no other prominent opponents of the Federal Reserve, such as William Jennings Bryan, Charles A. Lindbergh Sr., or Louis T. McFadden, are recorded as having died unexpectedly in a manner tied to their opposition. Bryan died in 1925 from natural causes, Lindbergh Sr. in 1919 from brain cancer, and McFadden in 1936 from a heart attack, years after the Fed’s establishment, with no evidence suggesting suspicious circumstances. Conspiracy theories circulating on platforms like X, such as claims about the Titanic, remain unsubstantiated and are not supported by historical records.
