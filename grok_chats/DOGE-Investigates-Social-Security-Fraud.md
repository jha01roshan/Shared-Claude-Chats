## Assistant

The Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE), led by Elon Musk, has been investigating the U.S. Social Security Administration (SSA) with a focus on identifying fraud, waste, and inefficiencies. Below is a summary of what DOGE has uncovered, based on available information, and public statements made by DOGE members, including former President Donald Trump.

### Findings by the DOGE Team

1. **Claims of Fraud Involving Deceased or Extremely Old Beneficiaries**:
   - DOGE initially claimed to have identified significant fraud in the Social Security system, particularly related to payments to individuals listed as extremely old or deceased. For example, they reported finding millions of individuals over 100 years old in the SSA’s NUMIDENT database, suggesting these were fraudulent beneficiaries. Former President Trump echoed this in a March 2025 address to Congress, claiming payments were being made to people as old as 360 years.[](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/doge-is-disrupting-social-security/)[](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2025/03/06/doge-is-driving-social-security-cuts-will-make-mistakes-acting-head-says-privately/)
   - However, these claims have been widely debunked. The SSA and experts clarified that many records in NUMIDENT lack birth dates or use default dates (e.g., 1875) due to outdated COBOL coding practices, and these individuals are not necessarily receiving benefits. The SSA stated that fraud represents only 0.00625% of its annual budget, far less than private sector thresholds.[](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/doge-is-disrupting-social-security/)[](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2025/03/24/social-security-fraud-doge-cuts-dead/)
   - DOGE also highlighted 3,095 loans issued by the Small Business Administration (SBA) in 2020-2021 to borrowers over 115 years old, totaling $333 million, implying misuse of Social Security data. However, no direct evidence linked these loans to SSA fraud.[](https://doge.gov/)

2. **Software and Licensing Inefficiencies**:
   - DOGE conducted a software audit at the SSA, identifying redundancies and unused licenses. Their findings included:
     - Four redundant survey tools costing between $118,000 and $1.8 million annually.
     - 10,000 UI Path licenses purchased, with only 5,011 in use.
     - 20,600 Tableau licenses purchased, with only 11,560 in use.
   - DOGE reported these as opportunities for cost savings, with fixes in progress, though specific savings figures were not detailed beyond a general claim of $232 million in cuts across SSA activities.[](https://doge.gov/)[](https://x.com/grok/status/1912627571461853627)

3. **Operational Disruptions and Data Access Issues**:
   - DOGE’s actions, including workforce cuts and office closures, have disrupted SSA operations. They proposed cutting 7,000 jobs (12% of the workforce) and closing dozens of field offices, leading to website crashes and service delays. These cuts were criticized as undermining the SSA’s ability to serve beneficiaries.[](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2025/apr/06/musk-doge-social-security)[](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2025/04/07/social-security-website-crashes-musk-trump/)[](https://www.epi.org/blog/what-is-doge-doing-to-social-security/)
   - DOGE staff accessed sensitive SSA databases, including the Master Beneficiary Record and NUMIDENT, raising concerns about privacy violations. Federal judges issued injunctions in March and April 2025, blocking DOGE from accessing non-anonymized personal data due to inadequate vetting and unclear justification.[](https://www.npr.org/2025/04/18/nx-s1-5369295/doge-data-social-security)[](https://fedscoop.com/doge-social-security-administration-restraining-order/)
   - A DOGE staffer, Antonio Gracias, claimed to have used SSA data to identify noncitizen voting in “friendly” states, alleging over 1,000 illegal votes in one state. These claims were questioned by voting experts for potential data errors and legal issues, and court records suggest DOGE should not have had access to such data.[](https://www.npr.org/2025/04/11/nx-s1-5352470/doge-musk-social-security-voting)

4. **Exaggerated Savings Claims**:
   - DOGE and Trump claimed potential savings of $500 billion or more from Social Security and other programs, but experts argue this is mathematically impossible without cutting benefits, which Trump promised not to do. Many of DOGE’s savings claims, such as $50.3 million from canceled SSA contracts, were overstated or lacked evidence.[](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2025/03/06/doge-is-driving-social-security-cuts-will-make-mistakes-acting-head-says-privately/)[](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2025/03/24/social-security-fraud-doge-cuts-dead/)[](https://www.epi.org/blog/what-is-doge-doing-to-social-security/)

### Public Statements by DOGE Members and Former President Trump

1. **Elon Musk**:
   - Musk has been vocal about alleged SSA fraud, calling the program a “Ponzi scheme” and claiming millions of deceased individuals were receiving benefits. In a March 10, 2025, interview, he stated there were “20 million people who are definitely dead marked as alive in the Social Security database.” These claims were refuted by SSA officials and former commissioners, who attributed them to misinterpretations of old records.[](https://www.cbpp.org/research/social-security/trump-administration-doge-activities-risk-ssa-operations-and-security-of)[](https://www.npr.org/2025/03/24/nx-s1-5337999/elon-musk-doge-social-security-cuts)
   - Musk also boasted on March 20, 2025, that DOGE was “cleaning up the fraud file” at SSA, though he provided no specific evidence.[](https://www.cbpp.org/research/social-security/trump-administration-doge-activities-risk-ssa-operations-and-security-of)
   - In a February 2025 social media post, Musk falsely claimed payments to “tens of millions” of deceased Americans, a statement later corrected by Acting Commissioner Leland Dudek.[](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2025/03/06/doge-is-driving-social-security-cuts-will-make-mistakes-acting-head-says-privately/)

2. **Former President Donald Trump**:
   - In a March 2025 address to Congress, Trump claimed DOGE had uncovered “shocking levels of incompetence and probable fraud” in the SSA, citing payments to extremely old individuals. He called for a Justice Department investigation, stating, “We’re going to find out where that money is going, and it’s not going to be pretty.” These claims were criticized as misleading.[](https://abcnews.go.com/Politics/doge-searching-social-security-payments-fraud/story?id=119564143)[](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2025/03/24/social-security-fraud-doge-cuts-dead/)
   - Trump repeatedly promised not to cut Social Security benefits unless fraud was proven, as noted in a February 18, 2025, Fox News interview with Sean Hannity. However, critics argue DOGE’s actions, like office closures, constitute backdoor cuts.[](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2025/03/06/doge-is-driving-social-security-cuts-will-make-mistakes-acting-head-says-privately/)[](https://www.propublica.org/article/doge-social-security-elon-musk-trump-closures-benefits)

3. **Antonio Gracias**:
   - On the April 4, 2025, “All-In” podcast, Gracias detailed DOGE’s cross-checking of SSA data with voter rolls in four states, claiming to have found “well over a thousand” noncitizens who voted. He said these cases were referred for prosecution, but voting experts questioned the accuracy and legality of the data use.[](https://www.npr.org/2025/04/11/nx-s1-5352470/doge-musk-social-security-voting)
   - Gracias publicized these claims at a March 30, 2025, rally in Wisconsin with Musk, amplifying them through conservative media and Musk’s X platform.[](https://www.npr.org/2025/04/11/nx-s1-5352470/doge-musk-social-security-voting)

4. **Acting SSA Commissioner Leland Dudek**:
   - Dudek, appointed in February 2025 after allegedly sharing data with DOGE, defended DOGE’s role in a February 21, 2025, Fox Business interview, stating it was “a critical part of President Trump’s commitment to finding fraud, waste, and abuse.” He downplayed claims of payments to deceased beneficiaries, clarifying that many old records did not indicate active benefits.[](https://www.foxbusiness.com/politics/acting-social-security-chief-opens-up-doge-claims-dead-people-receiving-benefits)
   - In private meetings, Dudek admitted DOGE was making decisions at SSA, describing them as “outsiders” who would “make mistakes” due to unfamiliarity with SSA systems.[](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2025/03/06/doge-is-driving-social-security-cuts-will-make-mistakes-acting-head-says-privately/)

### Critical Analysis

While DOGE’s stated goal is to root out fraud and inefficiency, their findings have been criticized as exaggerated or misleading:
- **Fraud Claims Overblown**: The SSA’s fraud rate is minimal, and DOGE’s focus on old records reflects a misunderstanding of legacy systems rather than evidence of widespread abuse.[](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/doge-is-disrupting-social-security/)[](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2025/03/24/social-security-fraud-doge-cuts-dead/)
- **Data Privacy Violations**: DOGE’s access to sensitive data without proper vetting has led to legal challenges, with judges ruling their actions likely violate privacy laws.[](https://www.npr.org/2025/04/18/nx-s1-5369295/doge-data-social-security)[](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/03/20/us/politics/musk-doge-social-security-records.html)
- **Operational Harm**: Workforce cuts and office closures have caused system outages and reduced access for vulnerable beneficiaries, contradicting DOGE’s efficiency goals.[](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2025/apr/06/musk-doge-social-security)[](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2025/04/07/social-security-website-crashes-musk-trump/)
- **Political Motivations**: Some DOGE actions, like Gracias’s voter fraud claims, appear to align with political narratives rather than substantiated findings.[](https://www.npr.org/2025/04/11/nx-s1-5352470/doge-musk-social-security-voting)

### Conclusion

DOGE’s investigation into the SSA has uncovered minor inefficiencies, such as redundant software licenses, but their high-profile fraud claims have been largely debunked or lack evidence. Public statements by Musk, Trump, Gracias, and Dudek have emphasized fraud and cost-cutting but often misrepresent the scale of issues or ignore SSA’s operational realities. Legal restrictions and expert critiques suggest DOGE’s approach has prioritized disruption over substantive reform, risking service delays and data security without delivering significant savings.
