**Here is a summary of major federal court cases involving COVID-19 policies (primarily vaccine mandates) during the Biden Administration (starting January 20, 2021).** These mostly centered on challenges to federal mandates for healthcare workers, large employers, federal employees, and contractors. Many were decided quickly via emergency applications to the Supreme Court.<grok:render card_id="48be8a" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">0</argument></grok:render>

### Key Supreme Court Rulings (2022)
- **Biden v. Missouri (consolidated with Becerra v. Louisiana)** (Jan. 13, 2022): The Supreme Court **upheld** the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) vaccine mandate for healthcare workers in Medicare/Medicaid-participating facilities (with medical/religious exemptions). It stayed lower-court injunctions.<grok:render card_id="f5ba94" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">0</argument></grok:render>
- **National Federation of Independent Business (NFIB) v. OSHA** (Jan. 13, 2022): The Supreme Court **blocked** the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) "vaccine-or-test" mandate for employers with 100+ employees. The Court ruled OSHA lacked authority for this broad public-health measure.<grok:render card_id="d0cfee" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">9</argument></grok:render>

### Major Lower Court Cases and Challenges
**Federal Contractor Vaccine Mandate (Executive Order 14042)**:
- Multiple states sued (e.g., *Georgia v. Biden*, *Louisiana v. Biden*, *Missouri v. Biden*, and others in various districts).
- Several district courts issued preliminary injunctions (some nationwide or limited). The Fifth Circuit affirmed a preliminary injunction in *Louisiana v. Biden* (Dec. 2022), criticizing it as an overreach of presidential power.<grok:render card_id="35913d" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">1</argument></grok:render>
- The mandate faced widespread blocks and was later withdrawn.

**Federal Employee Vaccine Mandate (Executive Order 14043)**:
- **Feds for Medical Freedom v. Biden** (S.D. Tex., then Fifth Circuit): District court issued a preliminary injunction (Jan. 2022). The Fifth Circuit (en banc) largely upheld challenges in 2023, but the Supreme Court later vacated rulings as moot after the mandate was rescinded.<grok:render card_id="f106b6" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">2</argument></grok:render>
- Other challenges in various circuits; mixed initial results, with some courts upholding and others blocking. Supreme Court ultimately vacated lower rulings as moot in 2023.<grok:render card_id="59fbbd" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">8</argument></grok:render>

**Other Notable Cases**:
- Challenges to the OSHA large-employer mandate were consolidated (e.g., *The Daily Wire v. OSHA* and state-led suits in the 5th/6th Circuits) before the Supreme Court blocked it.<grok:render card_id="cd8c0c" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">5</argument></grok:render>

### Context and Outcomes
- **Vaccine mandates** generated the most high-profile litigation. The Biden administration had some success with the healthcare worker mandate but faced significant losses or blocks on broader workplace, contractor, and federal employee mandates.<grok:render card_id="3293d4" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">18</argument></grok:render>
- Many cases became moot after mandates were rescinded or the pandemic evolved (e.g., Supreme Court vacating rulings in 2023).
- Lower courts (especially in the 5th Circuit) frequently issued injunctions, citing lack of statutory authority, federalism concerns, and overreach.<grok:render card_id="a6e9e7" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">46</argument></grok:render>
- Earlier COVID cases (e.g., eviction moratorium) spanned the Trump-to-Biden transition but continued into 2021.
