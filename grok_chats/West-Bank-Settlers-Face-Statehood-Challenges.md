## Assistant

### Overview of the New York Declaration and 21-Point Plan
The **New York Declaration** (adopted July 29, 2025, at a UN High-Level International Conference) is a multilateral commitment by over 140 countries to advance a two-state solution, ending the Gaza war and establishing a sovereign Palestinian state on 1967 borders (including East Jerusalem). It emphasizes halting Israeli settlement expansion, addressing settler violence, and implementing UN resolutions against illegal settlements, while calling for Palestinian elections and international protections.

The **21-Point Plan** (revealed September 26, 2025, as a U.S. proposal led by Steve Witkoff under President Trump) primarily outlines a Gaza ceasefire, hostage release, Hamas disarmament, and reconstruction via a transitional Gaza International Transitional Authority (GITA). It ties Gaza's future to a reformed Palestinian Authority (PA) for eventual reunification with the West Bank under a single elected leadership, explicitly barring West Bank annexation or new settlements to preserve a pathway to Palestinian statehood.

Both frameworks envision the West Bank (Occupied Palestinian Territory, or OPT) transitioning toward full Palestinian sovereignty, treating existing settlements as obstacles to viability. They do not provide affirmative protections or residency rights for the ~500,000 Israeli settlers, implying relocation or evacuation as statehood advances.

### Long-Term Issues Facing Settlers
As the West Bank evolves into Palestinian statehood under these plans, settlers face significant challenges rooted in legal, security, economic, and political realities. These stem from the declarations' rejection of settlements as illegal under international law (e.g., UNSC resolutions and the July 2024 ICJ Advisory Opinion), with no mechanisms outlined for their integration. Key issues include:

- **Forced Relocation and Evacuation**: 
  - Statehood on 1967 lines would require dismantling most settlements, as they occupy ~42% of the West Bank and fragment Palestinian territory.
