### FDTL Rules in India: Old vs. Current, and DGCA Revision Rationale

Flight Duty Time Limitations (FDTL) regulate pilot duty/rest to combat fatigue and enhance safety. India's Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) revised rules in January 2024 (phased implementation: Phase 1 July 2025, Phase 2 November 2025), aligning with global standards amid rising incidents.

| Aspect | Old Rules (Pre-2024) | Current Rules (2025) |
|--------|----------------------|----------------------|
| **Weekly Rest** | 36 hours | 48 hours (continuous; leave can substitute) |
| **Night Duty Definition** | 0000-0500 hrs | 0000-0600 hrs (extended by 1 hour) |
| **Night Landings per Week** | Up to 6 | Max 2 (no more than 2 consecutive nights) |
| **Max Flight Time** | 10 hrs (extendable to 12) | 10 hrs (extendable to 14 with conditions) |
| **Other** | Quarterly fatigue reports optional; no strict consecutive night caps | Mandatory quarterly reports; enhanced rostering for fatigue monitoring |

**Why DGCA Revised (Necessity)**: Pre-2024 rules, in place for over a decade, were outdated and misaligned with international benchmarks (e.g., FAA/EASA's stricter rest/night limits). Data from 2023-2024 showed rising fatigue-related incidents (e.g., near-misses, errors), exacerbated by India's post-COVID flight boom (domestic traffic up 20% YoY). A Delhi High Court directive in 2024 mandated updates after pilot union complaints. Revisions prioritize safety—fatigue contributes to ~15-20% of aviation errors globally—while incorporating stakeholder feedback (airlines, unions) and data analysis. Phased rollout allowed adaptation, though it caused short-term disruptions (e.g., IndiGo's 1,000+ cancellations in Dec 2025 due to crew shortages; temporary exemptions granted till Feb 2026). Long-term, it reduces accident risk in a market projected to be the world's 3rd largest by 2026.
