## Assistant

Section 10102 of Public Law 119-21 significantly expands SNAP work requirements, applying them to able-bodied adults aged 18 to 64 without dependent children and those living with children aged 14 or older. It removes exemptions for veterans, homeless individuals, and former foster youth aged 18 to 24, while introducing new exemptions for American Indians. Previously, work requirements applied to able-bodied adults aged 18 to 54 without dependents, requiring 80 hours per month of work or training to receive SNAP benefits for more than 3 months in a 36-month period. Starting in 2031, this would have applied only to those under 50 without the new law.

The section also limits states’ ability to waive work requirements, restricting waivers to counties with unemployment rates above 10 percent. Alaska and Hawaii can waive requirements if their state unemployment rate is at least 1.5 times the national rate and may request exemptions. Previously, states had broader authority to waive requirements in areas with high unemployment or insufficient jobs.

For further details, refer to the Congressional Budget Office’s estimated budgetary effects at www.cbo.gov/publication/61570 and supplemental distributional effects analysis at www.cbo.gov/publication/61367.

### Sanctuary Cities Policies and Healthcare

Sanctuary policies in ~600 U.S. cities/counties (e.g., New York City, San Francisco, Chicago, Los Angeles, Denver) **do not provide or mandate "free medical" for undocumented immigrants**. These policies primarily:
- Limit local law enforcement from inquiring about immigration status or honoring ICE detainers (unless for serious crimes).
- Prohibit using city resources for federal immigration enforcement.
