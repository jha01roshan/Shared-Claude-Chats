## Assistant

No, the vast majority of Catholic bishops worldwide are not homosexuals, based on available data and estimates. Claims suggesting otherwise—such as assertions that 80% or more of certain clergy groups are gay—largely stem from anecdotal reporting and investigative journalism rather than rigorous, representative surveys. I'll break this down below, focusing on the origins of the claim, existing data (mostly on priests, as bishop-specific surveys are scarce), and the limitations.

### Available Survey Data
There is no comprehensive, global survey on the sexual orientation of Catholic bishops (of which there are about 5,000-6,000 worldwide). Bishops are a small, elite subset of clergy, and self-reporting on such a sensitive topic is rare due to the Church's teachings on celibacy and homosexuality. Most data focuses on priests (around 400,000 globally), primarily in the U.S., and even that is limited by small sample sizes, self-selection bias, and anonymity issues. Key findings include:

- **2021 Survey of American Catholic Priests** (over 1,000 respondents, replicating a 2002 Los Angeles Times poll): About 17% of U.S. priests self-identified as homosexual in orientation (9% fully gay, 6-8% "somewhere in between but more on the homosexual side"). This is 4-5 times higher than the general male population (around 3-4%). However, rates are declining: Priests ordained before 2000 showed 11-15% homosexual orientation, dropping to 2-3% for those ordained after 2000. Projections suggest that by the 2040s, over 90% of U.S. priests will identify as mostly or entirely heterosexual. Younger priests also reported more conservative views on sexuality and higher celibacy adherence. ### Limitations and Context
- **No Survey Data for the Assertion**: The "vast majority" claim isn't backed by any peer-reviewed, representative survey. Martel's book and similar works rely on interviews, not statistical methods, and focus on the Vatican (a small, unique environment with about 800 clergy) rather than worldwide bishops.<grok:render card_id="fde78e" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation">
<argument name="citation_id">21</argument>
</grok:render> Reliable statistics are hard to obtain because homosexuality is stigmatized in the Church, and clergy are celibate, so orientation doesn't always imply behavior.

In summary, while homosexuality appears more common among Catholic clergy than in the general population, estimates for priests top out at 30-60% in some U.S. contexts, not a "vast majority," and are even lower in recent cohorts. For bishops worldwide, there's no solid data to support the claim—it's mostly speculative. If new surveys emerge, they could change this picture, but current evidence doesn't substantiate it.
