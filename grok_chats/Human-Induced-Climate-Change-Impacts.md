## Assistant

Climate change refers to long-term shifts in global or regional climate patterns, primarily driven by human activities since the Industrial Revolution. The overwhelming scientific consensus—greater than 99% among peer-reviewed studies and affirmed by bodies like NASA, the IPCC, and national academies of science worldwide—is that **Earth's climate is warming rapidly due to anthropogenic (human-caused) greenhouse gas emissions**, especially carbon dioxide (CO₂) from burning fossil fuels, deforestation, and industrial processes. No major scientific organization disputes this.

### Addressing Common Skeptic Arguments
While natural factors (e.g., solar variability, volcanic activity) influence climate, they cannot explain the rapid post-1950 warming—human emissions dominate. Past changes (e.g., Medieval Warm Period) were regional and slower, not global like today's. Claims of "no consensus" or data manipulation are unfounded; multiple independent reviews confirm >99% agreement on human causation.

### Future Outlook
The IPCC's latest assessments (AR6, 2021–2023) warn that without deep emissions cuts, warming could exceed 2–3°C by 2100, risking tipping points (e.g., ice sheet collapse). However, transitions to renewables are accelerating in many regions, offering pathways to limit warming if scaled urgently.

This is a well-established scientific reality, supported by decades of data from satellites, ice cores, and direct measurements. Mitigation (reducing emissions) and adaptation are critical to minimize risks.
