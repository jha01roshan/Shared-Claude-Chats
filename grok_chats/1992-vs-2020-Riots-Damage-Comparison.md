## Human

How do you compare the killing, injuries and property damage between the the 1992 Los Angeles riots (often called the Rodney King riots) and during the 2020 BLM protests. If you were to assign a factor of how much more overall damage the BLM riots caused - 4X?

---

## Assistant

**1992 Los Angeles Riots (Rodney King riots) vs. 2020 George Floyd/BLM protests (focusing on the violent/riot elements):**

The 1992 events were a concentrated, multi-day riot in the Los Angeles metro area (primarily South Central LA and surrounding neighborhoods) triggered by the acquittal of LAPD officers in the Rodney King beating trial. It lasted roughly 5–6 days.

The 2020 events began after George Floyd’s death and involved protests/riots across the U.S. (over 2,000 cities/towns, thousands of events in May–August alone). The vast majority (93–96%) were peaceful and caused no injuries or property damage, per data from ACLED and the Crowd Counting Consortium.<grok:render card_id="81df6b" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">20</argument></grok:render>

Here is a side-by-side comparison using the most consistently reported figures from contemporary sources (Wikipedia summaries, insurance industry reports, law enforcement aggregates, and news compilations like Forbes/Axios):

### Killings (Deaths)
- **1992 LA Riots**: 63 people killed (including ~10 by police/National Guard; the rest from homicides, fires, or riot-related incidents).<grok:render card_id="46705c" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">1</argument></grok:render>
- **2020 BLM/Geoge Floyd unrest**: 19 confirmed deaths in the peak period (May 26–June 8, 2020); broader estimates of ~25 Americans killed in protest-related/political unrest throughout 2020 (some by police, some by rioters/looters, some opportunistic).<grok:render card_id="465c98" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">15</argument></grok:render>

**Verdict**: 1992 was far deadlier (~2.5–3× more deaths) despite being confined to one metro area.

### Injuries
- **1992 LA Riots**: ~2,383–2,500 reported injuries (broad total, including civilians; exact police/civilian split not always broken out in summaries).<grok:render card_id="151590" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">1</argument></grok:render>
- **2020 BLM/Geoge Floyd unrest**: Civilian/protester injuries were reported at ~2.4% of events but not centrally aggregated like 1992 (studies note dozens to hundreds in major hotspots from less-lethal weapons, clashes, etc.). Police injuries were high: >2,000 officers injured nationwide (Major Cities Chiefs Association data for May–July 2020; some reports cite ~900+ in early weeks alone).<grok:render card_id="79e926" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">62</argument></grok:render>

**Verdict**: 1992 had higher overall reported injuries in a single localized event. 2020’s police injuries were substantial due to the national scale, but total civilian injuries appear lower or comparable relative to the number of events.

### Property Damage
- **1992 LA Riots**: ~$800 million–$1 billion total (1992 dollars); insured losses ~$775 million. Adjusted for inflation to ~2020 dollars: roughly $1.4–1.8 billion.<grok:render card_id="f61ce7" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">17</argument></grok:render>
- **2020 BLM/Geoge Floyd unrest**: $1–2 billion+ in insured losses from arson, vandalism, and looting (May–June peak alone; per Property Claim Services/Insurance Information Institute/Axios). This set the U.S. record for civil unrest, explicitly eclipsing the inflation-adjusted 1992 LA riots. Minneapolis–St. Paul alone saw ~$500 million+; damage hit 140+ cities.<grok:render card_id="7ac08f" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">17</argument></grok:render>

**Verdict**: 2020 caused more property damage (roughly 1–2× higher even after inflation adjustment), spread across the country rather than one city.

### Other Context (Arrests, Scope, Duration)
- **1992**: ~12,000 arrests; ~1,100 buildings damaged/destroyed; concentrated in LA over days.
- **2020**: 14,000–17,000+ arrests in the early period (higher overall); damage and violence in a small fraction of events but nationwide; sustained over weeks/months.

**Overall damage factor — was it 4X more for the 2020 BLM riots?**  
No. Property/economic damage was higher in 2020 (about 1–2× the inflation-adjusted 1992 total, per insurance data), making it the costliest civil disorder in U.S. history. However, killings were substantially lower (~1/3 as many), and total injuries were not dramatically higher given the vastly larger scale and duration.  

If "overall damage" weights human lives and injuries heavily alongside property (a reasonable way to think about riots), 1992 was more destructive on a per-event basis. The 2020 events caused greater total economic loss due to their nationwide reach, but not by a 4X margin — more like 1.5–2X economically, offset by fewer deaths. Sources across the spectrum (insurance firms, ACLED, major media) consistently describe 2020’s property toll as the new record but note far lower lethality than 1992’s concentrated violence.
