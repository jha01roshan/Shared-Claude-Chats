The authors of the **Cryptonite Weekly Rap** (a Substack newsletter by Anthony Perkins and team, focused on tech, crypto, AI, and Silicon Valley analysis) have expressed longstanding skepticism toward **OpenAI** and its CEO **Sam Altman**. Their doubts stem primarily from a 2023 piece reacting to Altman's brief ouster by the board, where they noted they had "been expressing our doubts about OpenAI prospects all along."<grok:render card_id="d514d6" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">33</argument></grok:render>

### Key Reasons for Distrust

**1. Flawed and convoluted corporate governance structure**  
OpenAI's hybrid nonprofit/capped-profit setup gave a small nonprofit board (lacking deep industry experience focused on shareholder value) outsized power over the for-profit arm, including the ability to fire the CEO. No one on the board, including Altman, directly owned shares, which the authors saw as problematic and deviating from standard Silicon Valley startup principles. They argued this structure was "riddled with structural, personal, and ideological differences" and made the company overly dependent on outside corporate partners like Microsoft, Amazon, and SoftBank, potentially distracting it from core priorities.<grok:render card_id="793a2d" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">33</argument></grok:render>

**2. Ideological conflicts and "doomer" influences**  
The authors highlighted an internal battle between the commercially oriented team (including Altman, Greg Brockman, and Mira Murati, who emphasized practical safeguards) and board members tied to the **Effective Altruism** movement. They viewed the latter as overly focused on extreme AI risk scenarios (e.g., "Terminator-type" outcomes where humans become "slaves to the robots"). This ties into broader criticisms of OpenAI's outputs, such as "preachy epilogues" on searches that "reek of Web2-style manipulation and censorship," repetitive or inaccurate responses, and a perceived ban on humor or certain topics (echoing Elon Musk's comments). The authors suggested "doomers" have no place leading the company and that the structure invited instability.<grok:render card_id="06c05d" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">33</argument></grok:render>

**3. Sam Altman's leadership style and focus**  
Altman comes across to them as a "nice enough fellow" but more of a **Y Combinator wheeler-dealer** than a "maniacally focused change-the-world entrepreneur." They questioned his ability to lead a pioneering AI company requiring intense dedication, pointing to his divided attention across projects like Worldcoin and other ventures. They suggested he step back into a VC or board role rather than CEO. In other coverage, they reference external accusations (e.g., from Elon Musk) of "repeated self-dealing," prioritizing profits over safety, and shifting from OpenAI's original nonprofit/open mission toward a closed, for-profit model.<grok:render card_id="c16788" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">33</argument></grok:render>

**4. Business model risks, overvaluation, and sustainability**  
Later articles criticize OpenAI's high cash burn, explosive scaling that "devours compute resources at a ruinous pace," slowing growth signals, and what they see as an overvalued "LLM house" (with Altman himself acknowledging in a leaked memo "rough vibes" and potential single-digit revenue growth). They predict market defeat by competitors like Google, possible forced sale (e.g., to Microsoft) at a loss for late-stage investors, and note Altman's own comment that "someone is going to lose a phenomenal amount of money." They also tie this to broader hype/bubble concerns in AI.<grok:render card_id="8ba021" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">34</argument></grok:render>

In summary, the Cryptonite authors view OpenAI as structurally unstable, ideologically conflicted, and poorly led for long-term success, with Altman embodying a deal-making style ill-suited to the demands of frontier AI development. Their tone is skeptical but analytical, often contrasting it with more "tried-and-true" Silicon Valley approaches or decentralized/Web3 alternatives they favor. These views appear consistent across their coverage of AI drama, though they have also acknowledged Altman's thoughtful public comments on AI risks in other contexts.
