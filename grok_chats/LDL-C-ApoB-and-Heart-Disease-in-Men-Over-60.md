### What "Per 1,000 Person-Years" Means

"Per 1,000 person-years" is a measure of incidence used in epidemiology to express the rate at which new events (like a first heart attack) occur in a population over time. It accounts for both the number of people at risk and the amount of time they’re observed. Here’s the concept:

- **Person-years**: This is the total time contributed by all individuals in a study while they remain at risk for the event. For example:
  - If 1,000 people are followed for 1 year each, that’s 1,000 person-years.
  - If 500 people are followed for 2 years each, that’s also 1,000 person-years.
  - If someone has the event (e.g., an MI) or drops out midway, they contribute only the time they were observed before that point.

- **Incidence rate**: The number of new events divided by the total person-years, then scaled to a standard unit like "per 1,000 person-years." So:
  - If 15 new MIs occur over 1,000 person-years, the incidence rate is **15 per 1,000 person-years**.

In practical terms, an incidence rate of 15 per 1,000 person-years means that, on average, 15 new heart attacks would occur if you followed 1,000 people at risk (men over 60 with no prior CVD, in your case) for one year—or followed 500 people for two years, etc.

   - So, for a single year—like age 60 to 61—it’s fair to approximate your personal risk as 15/1,000, or 1.5%.

2. **Cumulative Risk Over Multiple Years**:

     - Once someone has an MI, they’re no longer "at risk" for a *first* MI, so they stop contributing to the denominator (person-years). The rate applies only to those still event-free.
