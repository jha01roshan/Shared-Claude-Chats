## Assistant

Corn and spinach are both nutritious, but their nutrient profiles differ significantly due to their botanical and culinary classifications—corn as a starchy grain and spinach as a leafy green vegetable. Below is a comparison of their nutrient values per 100 grams of raw product, based on standard USDA data, focusing on key macronutrients, vitamins, minerals, and other relevant components. I’ll present the data concisely and then summarize the key differences.

### Nutrient Comparison (per 100g, raw)
| Nutrient              | Corn (Yellow, Sweet) | Spinach (Raw)       |
|-----------------------|----------------------|---------------------|
| **Calories**          | 86 kcal             | 23 kcal            |
| **Water**             | 76 g                | 91.4 g             |
| **Protein**           | 3.27 g              | 2.86 g             |
| **Carbohydrates**     | 18.7 g              | 3.63 g             |
| **Sugars**            | 6.26 g              | 0.42 g             |
| **Fiber**             | 2.0 g               | 2.2 g              |
| **Fat**               | 1.35 g              | 0.39 g             |
| **Vitamin A**         | 9 µg RAE            | 469 µg RAE         |
| **Vitamin C**         | 6.8 mg              | 28.1 mg            |
| **Vitamin K**         | 0.3 µg              | 482.9 µg           |
| **Folate**            | 42 µg               | 194 µg             |
| **Iron**              | 0.52 mg             | 2.71 mg            |
| **Magnesium**         | 37 mg               | 79 mg              |
| **Potassium**         | 270 mg              | 558 mg             |
| **Calcium**           | 2 mg                | 99 mg              |
| **Antioxidants**      | Lutein, Zeaxanthin  | Beta-carotene, Lutein |

### Key Differences
1. **Caloric and Macronutrient Content**:
   - **Corn** is higher in calories (86 kcal vs. 23 kcal) and carbohydrates (18.7 g vs. 3.63 g), making it a more energy-dense food, suitable as a staple grain. It has more sugars (6.26 g vs. 0.42 g), contributing to its sweeter taste.
   - **Spinach** is low in calories and carbs, with higher water content (91.4 g vs. 76 g), making it ideal for low-calorie diets. It has slightly less protein (2.86 g vs. 3.27 g) but comparable fiber (2.2 g vs. 2.0 g).

2. **Vitamins**:
   - **Spinach** is a vitamin powerhouse, particularly for:
     - **Vitamin A**: 469 µg RAE (from beta-carotene) vs. 9 µg in corn, supporting vision and immunity.
     - **Vitamin K**: 482.9 µg vs. 0.3 µg, crucial for blood clotting and bone health.
     - **Vitamin C**: 28.1 mg vs. 6.8 mg, aiding immunity and skin health.
     - **Folate**: 194 µg vs. 42 µg, important for DNA synthesis and pregnancy.
   - **Corn** has modest vitamin content but provides small amounts of B vitamins (e.g., thiamin, niacin), which spinach lacks in significant quantities.

3. **Minerals**:
   - **Spinach** excels in minerals:
     - **Iron**: 2.71 mg vs. 0.52 mg (though non-heme, less bioavailable).
     - **Magnesium**: 79 mg vs. 37 mg, supporting muscle and nerve function.
     - **Potassium**: 558 mg vs. 270 mg, aiding blood pressure regulation.
     - **Calcium**: 99 mg vs. 2 mg, beneficial for bones (though oxalate in spinach may reduce absorption).
   - **Corn** has lower mineral content but provides some phosphorus and zinc.

4. **Antioxidants**:
   - **Spinach** is rich in beta-carotene, lutein, and zeaxanthin, which support eye health and combat oxidative stress.
   - **Corn** contains lutein and zeaxanthin but in lower amounts; its antioxidants are less diverse.

5. **Other Considerations**:
   - **Corn** is a starchy crop, often processed (e.g., into cornmeal or syrup), which can alter its nutrient profile. It’s a staple in many diets but less nutrient-dense per calorie.
   - **Spinach** is typically consumed raw or lightly cooked, preserving its nutrient density. However, oxalates may inhibit mineral absorption, and cooking can reduce some vitamins (e.g., C and folate).

### Summary
- **Choose Corn** for: Higher energy (calories), carbohydrates, and a sweeter flavor, making it a good staple for energy needs. It’s less nutrient-dense but provides some B vitamins and antioxidants like lutein.
- **Choose Spinach** for: Low-calorie, nutrient-dense option with superior vitamins (A, C, K, folate) and minerals (iron, magnesium, potassium, calcium). It’s ideal for boosting micronutrients and antioxidants in a diet.
- **Complementary Use**: Pairing corn and spinach (e.g., in salads or dishes) can balance energy from corn with spinach’s micronutrient richness.
