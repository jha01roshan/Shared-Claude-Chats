## Assistant

Non-believers or strong skeptics of **AI** (particularly generative AI/large language models) often dismiss it using terms that echo the early crypto criticisms of being "useless" or "backed by nothing." The closest parallels aren't always a single "-less" word, but a few recurring phrases and metaphors capture the same dismissive vibe.

The most common ways skeptics characterize it include:

- **Just hype** or an **AI bubble** — by far the dominant framing right now (similar to crypto's "bubble" or "tulips" comparisons). Many call current AI enthusiasm an overinflated **bubble** that's bound to burst, with massive investments not matching real-world value or productivity gains. Critics point to it as financially unsustainable hype, much like dot-com or crypto manias.

- **Useless** (or effectively useless) — directly analogous to crypto's "useless." You'll see claims that generative AI is "effectively useless" for most practical high-value tasks, unreliable (hallucinates constantly), or only good for low-stakes toy uses. Some say it's "useless" in the same way pens are "useless" if misused — but the point is it's overhyped and doesn't deliver promised transformations.

- **Stochastic parrot** — this is probably the closest to a signature dismissive label from AI critics/academics. Coined in a 2021 paper, it means LLMs are just statistically sophisticated mimics ("parrots") that regurgitate patterns from training data without real understanding, meaning, or intelligence. It's like calling something fancy autocomplete or a "glorified autocomplete" — no true comprehension, just probabilistic word-spitting. Critics wield this to say it's fundamentally shallow, not intelligent.

- **Just autocomplete** / **glorified autocomplete** / **fancy autocomplete** — very common in everyday skepticism. Skeptics argue it's overhyped pattern-matching, not reasoning or intelligence — basically "just predicting the next word/token," with no deeper "backed by" substance like true cognition.

- Other variants: "nothing burger," "bullshit generator," "fake" intelligence, or "statistical approximation ≠ general intelligence."

So while there's no one perfect "-less" word that stuck like "worthless" or "baseless" did for crypto, the vibe lands on variations of **"meaningless"** (no real understanding), **"valueless"** (economically overhyped/useless for real work), or **"mindless"** (just parroting/mimicking without thought).

The stochastic parrot and bubble/hype labels are the ones that have become almost memetic among non-believers, much like "tulips" or "greater fool theory" was for crypto doubters.

## Assistant

**"Mindless"** correlates to AI skepticism in almost exactly the same way **"useless"** (or "backed by nothing") did for early crypto critics — it's a short, punchy, emotionally loaded dismissal that attacks the perceived **lack of real substance** or **core value** behind the technology.

Here's the direct parallel breakdown:

- **Crypto non-believers → "useless" / "backed by nothing"**  
  → Implies no intrinsic worth, no tangible asset or utility backing the price → just speculative hot air, greater-fool gambling, digital tulips with zero "real" foundation.

- **AI non-believers → "mindless"** (often as "mindless parroting," "mindless pattern matching," "mindless autocomplete," or "mindless bot")  
  → Implies no real intelligence, no understanding, no genuine cognition or reasoning → just statistical mimicry, probabilistic word-spitting, or glorified autocomplete with zero "mind" or comprehension behind it.

In both cases, the critic is saying:  
"This thing pretends to be revolutionary / valuable / intelligent, but strip away the hype/marketing and it's fundamentally empty / shallow / lacking the thing that would actually make it legitimate."

### Key ways "mindless" gets deployed against AI (echoing "useless" for crypto)
- **No true understanding** — Like crypto having no "real" backing (gold, government, utility), AI has no real grasp of meaning, truth, or concepts. It's accused of being a "stochastic parrot" (a very influential 2021 critique term) that mindlessly repeats patterns from training data without knowing what any of it means.
- **Just superficial imitation** — Crypto: "just numbers in a database, no actual money/product." AI: "just predicting the next token/statistically likely word, no actual thinking/insight."
- **Overhyped emptiness** → Both get called bubbles fueled by hype rather than substance. AI skeptics frequently say current LLMs are "mindless" tools good for low-effort slop (spam, filler content) but fail at anything requiring depth, reliability, or true creativity — much like how crypto was dismissed as useless for everyday payments or real economy integration.
- **Cultural/meme resonance** → "Useless" became a one-word gut-punch dismissal in crypto discourse. "Mindless" (especially paired with "parrot," "echo," or "autocomplete") fills that same role for AI: quick, memorable, and instantly conveys "this isn't what they claim it is."

### Examples from real discourse
- Skeptics call LLMs "mindless bots that cannot tell fact from fiction" or "mindless regurgitation of the internet."
- Reviews and critiques label the hype around AGI/superintelligence as built on "mindless machines" and "mindless myths."
- Everyday jabs: AI is "mindless pattern matching" with no soul/insight, just like crypto was "mindless speculation" with no backing.

So while crypto doubters zeroed in on **economic/foundational emptiness** ("useless"), AI doubters zero in on **cognitive/philosophical emptiness** ("mindless"). Both are ways to say: "This is fancy smoke and mirrors pretending to be something profound/valuable, but it's missing the essential core that would justify the excitement." 

It's the skeptic's shorthand for "not the real deal."
