### CCP Involvement in Cyber Warfare
The CCP oversees cyber operations through multiple entities:
- **People’s Liberation Army (PLA)**: Historically, units like the PLA’s Unit 61398 conducted cyber espionage, though roles have shifted. The newly formed PLA Cyberspace Force (2024) now centralizes cyber intelligence, defense, and attack capabilities.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwarfare_and_China)
- **Ministry of State Security (MSS)**: Since Xi Jinping’s leadership began in 2012, the MSS has taken a lead role in cyber espionage, managing advanced persistent threat (APT) groups like Volt Typhoon and APT40.[](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwarfare_by_China)
- **Private and Non-State Actors**: The CCP co-opts private companies, universities, and hackers, leveraging their skills for state-driven goals. Groups like APT41 blend state-sponsored espionage with criminal activities, often with tacit government approval.[](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/content-series/the-5x5/the-5x5-chinas-cyber-operations/)
- **Policy and Legal Frameworks**: The 2017 Cyber Security Law and the Cybersecurity and Information Leading Group (formed 2014) formalize state control over cyber activities, mandating cooperation from citizens and firms.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwarfare_and_China)

Operations are often conducted below the threshold of war to avoid direct confrontation, targeting rivals like the U.S., Taiwan, and regional neighbors to gain intelligence, economic advantage, or geopolitical leverage.

### Six Most Disruptive Tactics Used by the CCP
The CCP employs a range of cyber tactics, with the following being among the most disruptive based on impact and frequency:
1. **Cyber Espionage for Intellectual Property Theft**: APT groups target industries like AI, biotech, and aerospace to steal trade secrets, supporting initiatives like “Made in China 2025.” For example, Operation CuckooBees (2022) exfiltrated gigabytes of IP from global firms.[](https://www.csis.org/analysis/how-chinese-communist-party-uses-cyber-espionage-undermine-american-economy)
2. **Critical Infrastructure Infiltration**: Actors like Volt Typhoon pre-position malware in U.S. and allied infrastructure (energy, communications, water) to enable disruption during conflicts, such as a potential Taiwan scenario. This tactic threatens cascading societal impacts.[](https://www.cisa.gov/topics/cyber-threats-and-advisories/nation-state-cyber-actors/china)
3. **Supply Chain Attacks**: By compromising software providers or hardware, the CCP targets downstream users. Microsoft’s 2022 analysis noted China’s growing use of software vulnerabilities to scale attacks.[](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/content-series/the-5x5/the-5x5-chinas-cyber-operations/)
4. **Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) Attacks**: These overwhelm networks to disrupt services, often targeting government or media in rival states like Taiwan, which faced 2.4 million daily attacks in 2024.[](https://thesoufancenter.org/intelbrief-2025-january-10/)
5. **Disinformation and Influence Operations**: The CCP spreads propaganda to sway public opinion or destabilize democracies, notably during Taiwan’s 2024 elections. Collaboration with Russia on coordinated campaigns amplifies this tactic.[](https://thesoufancenter.org/intelbrief-2025-january-10/)
6. **Ransomware and Data Manipulation**: While less frequent, ransomware disrupts operations, and data poisoning (e.g., targeting AI datasets) undermines technological reliability. These are emerging threats tied to economic warfare.[](https://www.unav.edu/web/global-affairs/chinese-cyber-warfare-in-the-indo-pacific)

These tactics are disruptive due to their ability to erode trust, destabilize economies, and prepare for strategic escalation without kinetic conflict.

### CCP’s Higher Goal in Cyber Operations
The CCP’s cyber operations align with its broader ambition to achieve **global dominance and national rejuvenation** by 2049, the centennial of the People’s Republic. Specific goals include:
- **Economic Supremacy**: Stealing IP and targeting key industries accelerates China’s technological self-reliance and competitiveness, reducing dependence on the West.[](https://www.csis.org/analysis/how-chinese-communist-party-uses-cyber-espionage-undermine-american-economy)
- **Geopolitical Leverage**: Cyber capabilities deter adversaries (e.g., U.S. intervention in Taiwan) and weaken regional rivals, securing influence in the Indo-Pacific.[](https://www.boozallen.com/insights/cyber/chinas-cyberattack-strategy-explained.html)
- **Regime Stability**: Controlling information and countering foreign ideologies protect the CCP’s domestic authority, while external operations neutralize critics and dissidents.[](https://www.csmonitor.com/World/Passcode/Passcode-Voices/2017/0320/How-China-is-preparing-for-cyberwar)
- **Military Advantage**: Cyber warfare offsets U.S. conventional superiority, enabling asymmetric disruption of military logistics and infrastructure in potential conflicts.[](https://www.bankinfosecurity.com/experts-outline-chinese-cyberwarfare-tactics-motivations-a-18537)

Xi Jinping’s emphasis on “active defense” and “informationized warfare” underscores a strategy to use cyberspace to shape global norms, challenge Western dominance, and ensure the CCP’s unchallenged rule.[](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwarfare_and_China)

### Critical Perspective
While the CCP’s actions are well-documented, attribution in cyberspace is tricky, and China’s denials exploit this ambiguity. The U.S. and allies also engage in cyber operations, which China cites to deflect criticism. However, the CCP’s scale, integration of civilian hackers, and focus on economic theft distinguish its approach. Overhyping the threat risks escalation, but ignoring it leaves vulnerabilities unaddressed.
